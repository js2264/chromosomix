chromosomix 0.15.0

What changed in chromosomix 0.15.0, released on 17 October 2026.

There is no 0.14.0. A v0.14.0 tag and a GitHub release for it were made by hand, on the commit that merged #114, before npm version had bumped package.json: that build calls itself 0.13.0, its release carries no installers, and nothing was published to npm. Everything below is 0.15.0, cut the usual way, and the first version of the package on npm.

Added

Section titled “Added”

  • The site describes itself to language models and crawlers (§11). chromosomix.app/llms.txt, in the shape llmstxt.org gives one: what the app is, the formats it opens, how to write a link that opens a file at a place — every hash key a model is likely to want, with a map’s region 0-based and the 1D browser’s locus as displayed — how to embed it, how to run the command line, and where the documentation is. A test holds what it says to the app: each example link through the hash’s own reader, each value it lists against its list, each task against the registry and each store against the shelf, and it names no dev feature (§29.5). Beside it, llms-full.txt is the README as one file, generated at build time with its links made absolute; robots.txt welcomes every crawler but keeps the archived pins and pull-request previews out of search; and sitemap.xml lists the app and its validation and timing notebooks. New Open Graph tags give a pasted link a card to unfurl with.

  • chromosomix.app is written to be found by a search (§11). The page’s title and description now name what the app is searched by — genome browser for Hi-C, tracks and 3D models; Hi-C and Micro-C, .cool, .mcool and .hic — short enough for a results page to print whole, where the title said cool files, in the browser. And the app no longer writes its bare name over that title the moment it mounts: a search engine indexes the page as it stands after the script has run, so no title the page was given had ever reached one. On the web a tab with nothing open keeps the page’s title; with a file open it is still the file’s name and the app’s. A canonical link lists every address of the app — ?store=, ?config=, ?embed=1 — under the root; the <noscript> says what the app is, what it reads and where its source and llms.txt are, for a reader or a crawler without JavaScript, at the top of the page — it said only that JavaScript was needed, a screen’s height down a blank page, under the empty space the app mounts into; and the npm package’s description and keywords name the data and the analyses rather than metagenomics and metacool, a dev feature’s (§29.5).

  • chromosomix --version (§23). The first line is chromosomix and the version, with the commit the build was made from — the words every file the command writes opens with, so head -1 is what a pipeline records. Then the Node it runs on, with the platform, and the h5wasm and @gmod/bbi installed beside it, which read every map and track: both are ranges in package.json, settled at install, so two installs of one version can read a file through two builds of either. Answered wherever on the line it is written, as --help is. It printed the usage until now.

  • Every build carries the licences of what it compiles in (§22.4). THIRD-PARTY-NOTICES.txt beside the site’s, an archived pin’s, the desktop renderer’s and the package’s index.html, and beside the command in dist-cli/; a comment at the end of the standalone page, which may not be two files. It lists what the build carries — read off the bundle, workers included, so the CLI’s external h5wasm and @gmod/bbi are not listed and echarts’ zrender is — and reproduces each licence in full: echarts’ NOTICE and the d3 licence it embeds, HDF5’s inside h5wasm’s, and the material generators copied into the source from Lucide, Simple Icons, chromosight, matplotlib and ColorBrewer, whose upstream texts that are not npm packages are committed under licenses/. Every build’s check refuses one without them. Minifying had dropped every notice but three.js’s, and a tarball carried chromosomix’s own LICENSE alone. The README’s licence section says where each build keeps them.

  • A guided tutorial, from the landing page and About (§30). play the tutorial, on the landing page beside Load files and in About, walks a newcomer through a first session on a real map, one step at a time, with the window lightly greyed and the one control the step is about lit: the store drawer, opened from its header button on the lab’s store, then switched to 4DN’s; the IMR-90 Hi-C set 4DNES4296C5Q (a billion contacts), its accession in colour with a copy button, blinking when something else is typed; the set opened with its tracks, the General and Aesthetics columns shown, 250 kb and chr13; the map drawn as a correlation to show its plaid; the Analysis panel’s 2D, 3D and 1D tasks shown one group at a time, then Compartments run on chr13 alone, and its saddle; back to balanced, two Shift-Option drags across the squares the tutorial lights, down to a few domains at 10 kb, seen with O/E and 3 × 3 smoothing; Diamond insulation on chr13; the domains drawn as a lane under the map; Track correlation of the reader’s E1 and insulation against 4DN’s, the four ticked in one step; and the console. Each step moves on when what it asks for is done, however it was done, and shows a tick first. The card points at what to press, glides to the next thing, says what the step asks with an icon, and carries a progress bar cut into the tutorial’s ten parts, back, skip step and skip tutorial; ← and → step through it, and a press on the grey shows where to press instead. Skipped half way, the landing page and About offer to resume at the part the reader left; finished, to play it again. Also in the palette as Guided tutorial. Nothing opens by itself.

  • Tab goes on to the next field of its kind (§26.8). In any field edited in place, Tab now takes what was typed, as Enter does, and puts the caret in the next field of the same kind — Shift+Tab the one before — and past the last lets it go: a track’s name to the next track’s, down the browser’s lanes and through every stack round a map; a range’s bottom to its top and on to the next track’s bottom; a colour key’s low end to its high end, and on a deck to the next map’s key; a deck cell’s heading to the next cell’s; a row’s name on the Tracks, Metadata, Structures or Contact maps card to the next row’s; a tab’s name to the next tab’s; a mark’s last box to the next mark’s row; a sequence’s name on the editing stage to the next sequence’s; the 3D key’s bottom to its top. A value a field refuses keeps the caret, as Enter does. Tab used to jump to whatever button came next in the page — and on the editing stage it threw the typed name away. On the way, a colour key’s end opened and left as it was no longer fixes the bound at the number it showed, and its box opens on that number as the key prints it, -1.054, rather than on the float behind it, -1.0543182366, whose end was all the box had room for.

  • The installed app keeps its folders, and a saved workspace finds its files in them again (§20.6). In the desktop app — or the site installed as an app — a connected folder is kept across restarts, and a workspace saved there records which folder each of its local files was in and where: reopened, it opens the map and its tracks from the folder with nobody asked for a file, and on another machine its note names the folder to connect, with a button that does. The desktop app now lets its own page re-read what the user chose, and nothing else.

  • The tracks’ names beside the map, in its corners, with the name column on (§8.8). The 1D browser’s name column switch (u) is now at the end of the all tracks row of the map’s Tracks card too, and moves the names beside a map as well: on, each stack’s names stand in the corner that follows it clockwise — left of the tracks under the map, below the ones on its right, above the ones on its left, right of the ones above it — rather than over the start of each track, where a name and its range cover the first stretch of the signal. A short name sits in the margin beside the map’s own labels and a long one runs out over the corner, which grows to fit (wrapping a name past 150 px); the range sits under its name, and a press on either still opens its caret, a right click the lane’s menu. Figures saved from the map carry them where the screen does. The switch is off to begin with, so the names are written over the tracks as they always were; for a while the corners were the only place they went, with no switch on the map’s card to put them back.

  • Each cell of the deck can be on a sequence of its own (§16.3). Every map’s heading now says which sequence it shows, as a quiet picker beside its name: pick V on one cell and that map leaves the chain and reads the whole of V at its own resolution while the others stay on IV — its window is remembered with the map, and the × on it puts the map back on the others’ window. On the first map the picker goes where the locus box would, and every map following it goes along.

  • A scalogram’s lane reads in distances (§18.12). Its range said 0 – 16.7, the logarithms of the reach, and its floor sat at one base pair, so the lower half of the lane was a band no value could reach. It now says 1 kb – 104 kb, starts at the power of ten below its data, and carries an axis of its own: a faint dashed rule at 10 kb, 100 kb, 1 Mb… with the distance written at the lane’s far end, stepping round the names rather than through them — beside a map, in the browser and in a saved figure. Any log2 lane gets the rules and the floor; one whose values are lengths gets them in kb and Mb.

  • A box over the Analysis column narrows the task list (§18.1). filter 25 tasks…: every word typed must be in a task’s title, its blurb, its card’s heading or its other names — each task now carries the words a reader looks for it by, TAD for insulation and the directionality index, pileup for APA, A/B and eigenvector for the compartments, decay for P(s). A card with nothing left goes, nothing left at all is said, Escape gives the list back and Enter opens the first task left that can run.

  • Every step back, as a list, from a chevron beside the header’s back button (§5.5). It names each step behind the one on screen — the window, a block of two, the whole genome, a table — with the resolution, the O/E or the comparison it set beside it and the file where that is another one, and a row goes straight there, as that many presses of back would. The app keeps a record of the entries it writes, which the browser does not list, in the window’s own storage, so the list survives a reload.

  • Two windows typed in a locus box open the block between them (§5.4, §26.1). chr1:1,000-2,000 chr2:3,000-4,000, or the same with x, | or × between, opens chr1 against chr2 — rows, then columns — in the region view’s box, the whole genome’s and the Chromosomes table’s; everything after the first window used to be dropped without a word. On a block of two windows the box shows both, IV:1,000,001-1,200,000 × V:1-200,000, where it showed the rows alone and Enter on it threw the columns away.

  • A row of the Files column dropped on Load a map into the lower triangle is the second map in the square (§5.7, §20.5). The Contact maps card’s slot took only a file from the desktop: a row dragged out of a connected folder lit it and fell in for nothing. The row’s file is read at the drop, through its folder, with the credential the folder lists with and the name a Drive or a Dropbox gives it; on a session with no map it is the first map. The slot no longer lights for the card’s own grips or for text dragged across it.

  • The validation and the timings are published with every release, at https://chromosomix.app/validation/ and https://chromosomix.app/timings/ (§18.10). A new workflow, .github/workflows/notebooks.yml, installs every reference on a fresh runner at the versions the notebooks were written against — notebooks/requirements.txt and requirements-yapc.txt pin them — renders both notebooks and publishes them beside the app, each with its tables beside it: every command, every verdict and every timed run. It runs when a release is published, attaching both pages, every verdict and every timed run to the release as well, and on demand, with the number of timed runs and whether to publish; every run keeps what it rendered as an artifact, even when the timings fail. Each page opens on when it was rendered, from which version, commit and release, by which run, on what machine, and closes on how long it took, and the two link to each other. The service worker leaves both addresses to the network, as it does archived builds, so a visitor who has installed the app reaches the page rather than the app — once their copy has taken this version’s update; until then, an older worker answers there with the app.

  • chromosomix boost writes Boost-HiC’s map of each sequence (§6.5). The map an oversample draws a sequence’s new contacts from, which nothing showed: each sequence’s stored counts, the square an oversample hands Boost-HiC, through the same port, written as Boost-HiC’s main.py writes its own — the bins its outlier filter keeps and the boosted map over them, in SCN units, one square TSV a sequence. --sequences names some and --alpha another exponent; a sequence past 3,000 bins is refused by name, as an oversample refuses it. Over three mouse chromosomes at 100 kb it keeps the same bins as Boost-HiC’s Python and agrees with it within 5 × 10⁻¹⁰, ten times faster on chr1.

  • Copy beside PNG and PDF on every task’s figure (§18.13). It puts the figure’s PNG — the same 300 dpi redraw the PNG button saves — on the clipboard, to paste into a slide or a message without a file in between, and says in the corner what was copied and at what size.

  • The lab’s store opens the Chromosomix paper, five tabs, a panel each (§24.2, §24.3). The Chromosomix paper replaces Chromosomix, Fig. 1 under Papers. Fig. 1c is 4DN’s mouse ES-cell Hi-C at five scales side by side, each map at its own rung and on its own window — the genome at 2.5 Mb, chromosome 3 as the correlation of its observed/expected at 250 kb with E1 and A/B under it, chr3:27–37 Mb at 10 kb with its insulation and borders, the Sox2 locus as observed/expected at 5 kb with its genes, CTCF and loops, and Sox2 with its control region at 2 kb with H3K27ac and H3K4me3 — and under each map the model the figure inferred at that scale, coloured by a track. Fig. 1d is chr3:32–36 Mb at 10 kb with its insulation, borders, CTCF and genes, beside chromosome 3’s model coloured by CTCF. Fig. 2a is C. necator’s first chromosome, its origin in the middle, over the decay along the diagonal and then along both arms; Fig. 2b the B. subtilis wild type, ΔparAB and six times after ParB is induced; Fig. 2c P. aeruginosa WT, SMC-ScpAB– and MksBEF– as the correlation along both arms, with each strain’s E1 under its map and the wild type’s RNA-seq (PAK at T0, GEO GSE76513, drawn to 30 CPM) under its own. A deck keeps one look a map, so a panel that reads one map several ways opens on one of them and the map’s score picker has the rest. The files are one folder, demo/store/chromosomix_paper/, a subfolder an organism, the bacterial maps and the RNA-seq copied there from where the lab keeps them so the card names none of those places; scripts/make_rsg_store.py derive computes the three E1 tracks with chromosomix compartments, as the zoo does. The bucket needs scripts/publish-store.sh again.

  • A model coloured by a track can run from white to the track’s colour, and its key’s ends can be typed into (§25.5). White to the track’s colour joins the colour-scale picker under a track, so a model coloured by H3K27ac is drawn in its lane’s orange. A click on either end of the key’s range puts a caret in it, as on a lane; the number is written to the lane’s limits, so the lane follows, and an arrow beside the range puts the track back on autoscale.

  • Find a motif searches a genome too large to hold, and searches the sequences ticked (§8.8, §18.8). A FASTA opened by its .fai — a mouse or human genome — or a .2bit was refused by the search for not being held in memory; it is now read by range sixteen megabases at a time, each piece scanned and let go, with an overlap one base short of the motif so a site across a seam is found once, and the sites are the ones a search of the whole file finds. The form takes the sequences to search as tick boxes, all of them to begin with; fewer are named in the lane (GATC on chr1) and get rows of their own. Over a reference read by range the line beside Run prices the read — every base of the ticked sequences, in bytes, and over a network in requests — and a Stop is seen between two pieces.

  • The chromosomix command opens a --sequence by its index (§23.1, §8.21). A FASTA with its .fai beside it, or a .2bit, is read by range as the browser reads it, so a mammalian reference is a reference from a shell: Node’s readFile refuses a file past 2 GiB, which was every plain mouse or human FASTA. One past it with no index is refused by a sentence naming samtools faidx.

  • Marks hidden and shown, one or all of them, on every map, track and model at once (§8.1). Each row of the Manual annotations card has an eye, as a track’s row does, and the list a heading, MARKS, with how many are hidden and one eye for all of them over the rows’ eyes; A is that eye from the keyboard, and the palette’s Manual annotations switch. A hidden mark leaves the map (square or turned), the lanes and their anchors, the 1D browser, a tracks cell and the 3D model — its bead, its chord, its colouring — and figures, and stays on the card to be shown again. Hiding every mark and showing them again brings back exactly what was drawn: a mark hidden from its own row stays hidden. A double-click on an eye shows that mark alone, and a second brings the rest back. Every right-click menu — the map’s, a lane’s, the 1D browser’s and the model’s — names the marks under the pointer, Hide this line and the like, measured on the ink as it is drawn, and offers Hide every mark or Show the marks. Hidden marks are kept with the session, a link and a reopen at another resolution, and the card’s BED and BEDPE still carry them.

  • Inter-arm alignment draws what its detrending leaves, and the two decays it divides by (§18.8). The profile after detrend — the same cells over the decay along both arms — is a curve beside the measurement’s, the two opening on one ordinate so a flat control reads as flat; and a log–log figure gives the two expectations, the ordinary decay along the diagonal and the decay along both arms within an arm and across them from the secondary diagonal, log-binned as P(s)’s expected curve is (task version 7).

  • A table’s names can be a scatter’s abscissa, and the scatter its least-squares line (§18.13). A text column whose cells write numbers — the all-maps table’s Map, where the maps are +parB 15 min, +parB 30 min — is offered as the abscissa, each row at the first number in its name and a name with none left out; least squares draws the fitted line, named in the legend by its equation, r² and n, so the slope of a time course goes out in its PNG and PDF. The line is fitted to the points in the abscissa’s window and refitted as it moves, so a box drawn across the points that are under way fits those alone.

  • Half a window upstream and downstream, beside go on a region (§5.4). Two arrows straight after the locus box’s go move the window half its own width along the sequence, re-read as a navigation, so a reader at a locus can walk the chromosome without retyping it. They slide in to stop at an end and grey once there, on the whole sequence and on a block of two sequences, and name the window they would open in their tooltips.

  • Back and forward in the header (§5.5). The browser’s own back and forward as two buttons, past a divider after the four panels’ toggles, greyed where the browser says there is nowhere to go.

  • A model’s PDF is lit, as its PNG is (§25.7). Every bead is a shaded sphere, a PDF radial shading from a highlight to a shadowed rim, and the tube and the sticks are drawn band by band from the rim in shadow to a highlight on the side the screen’s key light comes from, so the fibre reads as a solid on paper rather than as flat ribbons. Still paths and shadings and nothing else, smooth at any zoom; the fog fades the shading with the rest. The file is larger for it.

  • Every colour swatch opens one picker of the app’s own, hex first, with the colours last picked (§26.6). The six digits are the box, ready to type or paste into; the square and the hue bar can be dragged across without the picker closing — the platform’s closed after the first step, because the card under it was rebuilt — and the lane follows the drag. Under them, the last sixteen colours picked anywhere in the app, one click each; an eyedropper where the browser has one.

  • A bar under the 1D browser’s tracks makes them all taller or shorter together, each in proportion (§5.8). The deck cell’s height bar in look, under the last track: dragged, every track’s height is scaled by one factor, so their sizes relative to each other stay as they were; Escape mid-drag puts them back, a double click fits the whole stack to the window, and the arrow keys step it. Only in the 1D browser on its own — not beside a map, nor in a deck. The canvas now gives its height back when the tracks are made shorter, and Stretch the tracks (h) fills the window rather than a canvas a tall stack had grown.

  • The map says how to zoom into a region while the pointer is over it (§8.9). Under the key in its top-right corner — or in the corner itself with the legend off — a line or two of plain text: Alt + drag to zoom into a region and Shift + Alt + drag to zoom along the diagonal, or ⌥ and ⇧ on a Mac. One line under the diagonal lock, which already pins the box, a stretch on a turned map, and no Shift line on a block the diagonal does not cross. Gone when the pointer leaves, never in an export, and not on a touch screen. The 1D browser says Alt + drag to zoom into a stretch, or drag along the ruler under its ruler.

  • Base modifications from a BAM’s MM/ML tags (§8.13, §8.20). A pile lane’s colour by offers base modification: the reads are drawn grey and each 5mC, 5hmC, 6mA or other call sits at its own base, red where modified and blue where not, as opaque as the caller was sure — for the code chosen from the ones the file’s reads carry, at a probability threshold the reader sets. A hover on a call names its code, probability and base, and a read’s card counts its calls. The depth row can draw the percentage modified instead, base by base, from the same reads under the same filters: a bedMethyl made in the browser. Reverse-strand reads are counted on their sequence as read, . and ? modes are told apart, two codes on one base, ChEBI codes and the N wildcard are read, and calls in soft clips and insertions are dropped. A simulated nanopore BAM over yeast chrIV is in demo/yeast/ to try it on.

  • Every called set is drawn on the map and as a lane (§18.7, §8.1, §8.16). Peaks, insulation’s boundaries and domains, the DI’s borders and domains, A/B compartments, Pattern detection’s borders, origins, sites, flagged contigs and MAGs used to arrive as an overlay on the map only — beside a map a peak set could not be read against the coverage it was called on. Each now arrives as two rows of the Tracks card, linked: the overlay, and a lane of segments placed directly under the row it is read against — the peaks under their bigWig, the A/B segments under E1, the boundaries under the insulation track. Each row’s settings and the drawer carry on the map and as a lane, which are the two rows’ eyes and are remembered as they are (§8.6). A loop set’s lane is its anchors, and starts off; with no map — the 1D browser — the lane comes alone. Segments carry a name — A/B, peak_12, domain_3, boundary_40 — into the BED, and a lane can be coloured by its name column, which a compartment lane is from the start.

  • The stage’s corner dot plot zooms, grows, and answers the map — a dev feature (§27.3). The wheel zooms it about the pointer, a drag moves it and a double click shows it whole, as the dot plot’s own view does, the lines sharp at any zoom; a grip at its lower-left corner sizes it, kept on the map and remembered with the file. Hovering a contig’s name on a pieces bar, or its row on the sequences card, lights its lines in the plot and dims the rest; hovering a line in the plot lights its contig on the map — dashed on the diagonal, bracketed on both bars, its name in the accent.

  • A FASTA’s six reading frames, in any of NCBI’s genetic codes (§8.8). Reading frames in a sequence row’s settings draws six rows under its residues — +1, +2, +3 over −1, −2, −3, anchored to the sequence’s first base so they do not move with the window — each codon a shaded box with its amino acid in it, starts green and stops red; further out the starts and stops alone as ticks, to 24 bases a pixel. The code beside it offers every NCBI translation table, so a Mycoplasma’s frames can be read in table 4, where TGA is tryptophan and not a stop. A hover over a row names the codon and what it codes for; both settings are remembered with the row.

  • A 3D model coloured by a BED’s names, A red and B blue (§25.5, §8.16). Every feature set with a name column — a BED4 or wider, a bigBed, a task’s segments — is offered under the model’s colour by as · names: each bead takes the name that overlaps it most, so a compartment call colours the model A and B, and a bead under no feature stays grey. The key lists each name with a swatch that is a colour picker; the colour is kept on the set’s row with the session, so it is that name’s colour on every model coloured by the set and on the set’s own lane, which can now be coloured by name too. Exactly A and B start red and blue, anything else on the categorical palette by base pairs covered; past twenty-three names the rest are one other the key counts.

  • Every map drawn to one number of contacts (§6.5). A box labelled contacts ends the breadcrumb, showing the map’s own total in grey — the sum of cooler info, 179,175,551 for the yeast micro-C.   • A second way to build a model, and it is now the default: pastis-NB (§25.6). Model from the map gains a method control offering the two positions the literature actually takes on what a contact count is. ShRec3D, which is what the task did before and still does when picked, reads a count as a measurement of a distance, c^(−1/α), fills in the pairs the map never counted so the matrix is complete, and places the beads by classical scaling and weighted SMACOF. pastis-NB (Varoquaux, Ay, Noble & Vert 2014; Varoquaux, Noble & Vert 2023) reads a count as a count — negative binomial, with a mean of β·d^(−α) — and returns the model that makes the counts most probable.

    Two things follow, and they are why it is the default. A zero becomes evidence rather than a problem: 0^(−1/α) is infinite, so ShRec3D has to either drop such a pair or invent a distance for it, and the invention is the one step of that pipeline that is a guess. Under a likelihood a zero is a reading — probably far, and past a point indifferent to how far, which is exactly what a pair a shallow experiment missed should say — so nothing is filled in at all. On a simulated map that is three-quarters zeros, the classical start orders the pairs at ρ = 0.22 and the likelihood brings the same beads to ρ = 0.92. And a count’s precision is in the likelihood itself, so a pair seen ten thousand times holds its beads ten thousand times as firmly with nothing weighted by hand — which is why weight pairs by is hidden under this method rather than being asked twice.

    It reads the raw counts, and the balancing goes into the mean. Not a preference — a necessity, and the one thing about this that is easy to get quietly wrong. A balanced value is a count times two weights, and overdispersion is a statement about a count: variance > mean compares a squared quantity with a linear one, so scaling the data scales the two sides differently and a balanced map is never found overdispersed, whatever it actually is. Handed balanced values the negative binomial would silently collapse back into a Poisson, on nearly every real file. So the counts stay raw and the weights go where pastis puts them — into the mean, μ = β·bᵢ·bⱼ·d^(−α) with bᵢ = 1/wᵢ. The coverage is still corrected; it is corrected in the mean rather than in the data, and the run says so in a note and in the table. A bin the balancer rejected is left out, as the balanced view of the same map leaves it out. The reported Spearman ρ is taken against raw/(bᵢbⱼ) — which is the balanced value — so it stays the number Model versus map would give and does not score the model on coverage it was right to remove.

    The dispersion is fitted from your map, per genomic separation, by the method of moments over log-spaced separation bins — pastis-NB’s one claim over pastis-PM2 being that overdispersion is not one number for a map but grows as the counts thin out with distance. The whole of the negative binomial over the Poisson turns out to be a single factor: the pull on a pair is the Poisson’s own μ − c, shrunk by r/(r + μ). Where a map shows no overdispersion the run says so in as many words — none: these counts are Poisson, so this is pastis-PM2 — rather than implying a parameter it did not measure. On a helix whose far counts were corrupted, the fitted dispersion holds the near diagonal about twice as well as a flat Poisson does.

    The fit table now names the method and reports what that method has to report: for pastis-NB, the mean’s law, how many zeros were used as evidence, the dispersion near the diagonal and far from it, the MDS start, the likelihood steps and evaluations, why the optimiser stopped, the final −log L and the fitted β. Both methods still end with Spearman’s ρ against the counts.

    The cost, said before you press Run. The estimate prices the method you picked: a likelihood evaluation is about 2.3 SMACOF passes’ worth, and a step is a line search of just over one evaluation, so pastis-NB is roughly half again the wall clock of ShRec3D for the same step count. Memory is unchanged — two n × n matrices either way, because the likelihood adds vectors and not squares: L-BFGS at depth eight keeps twenty vectors of 3n + 1 doubles, six megabytes beside a twelve-thousand-bead gigabyte, where a full quasi-Newton method’s Hessian at that size would be thirty terabytes. The whole addition is 9.7 kB minified, 3.5 kB gzipped, in the chunk the analysis panel already loads.

    Mechanics. structure/negbinom.ts is the model — dispersion, objective, gradient, fit — and structure/lbfgs.ts is a limited-memory BFGS with a strong-Wolfe line search (Nocedal & Wright 3.5/3.6), the optimiser pastis itself reaches for through SciPy. Both are pure over arrays, and both are generators: the likelihood yields between evaluations rather than between steps, because a line search is several passes over the pairs and each is as long as a SMACOF step, so the bar moves and a cancel always has somewhere to land. pastis-NB is started from the MDS model ShRec3D would have given — sixty SMACOF steps, fixed, since it has only to land in the right basin — which is pastis’ own initialisation, so the new method stands on the old one rather than replacing it. α stays the reader’s control under both, and is not fitted, so a model made here and a model made by either published tool mean the same thing by it. What is refused is named in §25.8: no restarts, no alternation between dispersion and structure, no physical unit.

  • An install of the package downloads what the command runs, and no more (§22.2). echarts and three are devDependencies now: both are compiled into dist/, and the command line loads neither, so as dependencies they and zrender under them were 90 MB of the 116 MB that npm install -g chromosomix put on disk. The README installs the CLI from npm — npm install -g chromosomix, or npx chromosomix@<version> for a pinned release — and keeps the clone and npm link for trying a change before it is released.

  • Compartments finds its components by Lanczos iteration, in a third of the time (§18.8). Each component was found by power iteration, which converges at the ratio of the two largest eigenvalues: five hundred products of the matrix a component, seven-tenths of the task’s time on the validation’s mouse map at 100 kb — and where two eigenvalues are close in size not enough, so chr14’s third component came out a mixture of two eigenvectors. The replay that writes a chart into a PDF or a PNG flattened every gradient to its middle stop, and a matrix chart’s continuous colour bar is one: every exported pileup, saddle plot and cis/trans square had a key of one orange rectangle beside a picture in a hundred colours. The pin now follows the picture: a map, a browser stage or a deck’s grid keeps it, and a drop zone and a table of chromosome lengths do not.

  • The About dialog no longer ends with a sentence about a list it does not have. A site that publishes no build manifest drew This deployment does not publish a list of past builds. under a picker that was not there; the row takes itself away instead. Fixed

Section titled “Fixed”

  • A release tag that is not the package’s version builds and publishes nothing (§22.3). The release workflow’s first job now refuses a v* tag unless it is v and the version package.json carries, and every other job waits on it. v0.14.0, made by hand with GitHub’s release form on a commit still versioned 0.13.0, had the workflow set about 0.13.0 installers and pack chromosomix@0.13.0 for npm, which only a refused token kept off the registry for good. A release body that would be too long for GitHub — this one’s changelog section is 290 kB — is posted as each entry’s bold lead with a link to the section in full, rather than failing the release at its last step. And docs/deploy.md no longer tells anyone to create a release by hand: it still said the installers were built by gh release create, which the workflow stopped waiting on when it moved to the tag; it now says what a tag sets off, and what the npm token needs.

  • A reader that stops reading no longer kills the command (§23). chromosomix tasks | head -1, or a less quit early, closed the pipe under the command, and Node’s report of the next write — EPIPE, EOF on Windows — went unheard: a stack trace and exit 1 the moment head had its line, and under insulation map.cool 2>&1 | head -1, where the progress on stderr is what closes, a task that died before writing a single file. Both streams now let the reader go; what was left to print is dropped and the command finishes what it was doing — all seven of that run’s files, identical to an unpiped run’s — and exits with the status it would have had.

  • A track still reading goes with the map to another resolution (§8.6). A change of resolution carried a map’s tracks to the new one only once they had loaded: picking another resolution in the second or two after a store’s session opened, while its tracks were still being read, lost every one of them, there and for good. A track on its way is now carried too, and read again at the new resolution.

  • A box is washed over the tracks beside a map, as it is in the 1D browser (§8.1). A stretch highlighted in the browser — a rectangle drawn on the tracks — showed there as a wash over every track and on the map as the square on its diagonal, but beside the map it turned into two small arrowheads under the tracks. The tracks beside a map now carry the same wash, over every lane of every stack, both stretches of a box off the diagonal, and in the saved figure; the arrowheads under the tracks are a loop’s alone, and a right-click anywhere inside a wash offers to hide its box.

  • Every task measures a resolution you added, as the map draws it (§3.4, §18.4). With the map at 3 kb* — the file’s 1 kb bins summed three at a time — a task still read the 1 kb rung underneath, so an insulation track or a P(s) came out at 1 kb under a picture at 3. The analysis worker now reads the map summed to the resolution on screen: counts exactly, as the rung the file would store (a 1 kb rung summed to 4 kb reads as the file’s own 4 kb, pixel for pixel), and weights by the harmonic mean of the bins’ weights. The drawer says 3 kb* bins, a result goes out of date when you step off the resolution, and the console’s command carries --coarsen 3000, which chromosomix now takes too.

  • Every export is on white in the dark theme too (§7.2, §16.5). A map’s PNG and PDF, a deck’s, a copied map, lane or track, and the box behind an exported key were laid on the page’s own colour, so in the dark theme they came out on near-black under ink chosen for white; and a task’s figure enlarged into the stage dialog, which follows the theme, exported dark from there. Every picture that leaves the app is now laid on white and redrawn in the light palette’s ink, wherever the figure was mounted when the button was pressed.

  • Split after a ratio is drawn on the counts’ own scale again (§7.5). A ratio takes the transform, the ramp and the clip — log2 and the diverging ramp — and was to give them back when switched off, but a change of comparison rebuilds the map, and the panel that would have given them back was the one torn down: the split after a ratio stayed on log2 and blue to red, and so did a map whose second triangle was hidden under a ratio. What a derivation took over is now carried to the map built in its place, so ratio → split and ratio → difference → split end on the scale before the ratio, log10 and the default ramp on a balanced cooler; a ratio reached by a link, a reload or Back gives back the file’s defaults. Closing the first file of a comparison and swap sides carry the scale from before the ratio too.

  • APA’s track aggregates stay unticked once unticked (§18.2, §18.8). With every track unticked, the next run ticked the starred one back on: the drawer hands the form its context around a run, and an empty list read as one nobody had answered. A list nobody has touched still opens on the starred track; one the reader emptied is kept empty.

  • Track correlation’s windows are their exact means, and a mammalian genome at 100 bp fits in memory (§18.8). A window’s value was taken from whatever zoom level its size selected for drawing, and each record weighted by its whole width rather than by how much of the window it covers: a 17 kb record laid whole over a 10 kb window. On 4DN’s two mouse ATAC-seq replicates at 10 kb that gave r = 0.808 where the windows’ own means give 0.884. Every track is now read at its own resolution, a megabase at a time, and each record weighted by its overlap — pyBigWig’s stats(…, exact=True) — so the two replicates read 0.884, and on chr19 they and a Hi-C insulation score agree with pyBigWig’s exact means and SciPy to 5 × 10⁻⁷ at 100 bp, 1 kb and 10 kb, by both methods. A window was also an object each and a sequence one request: a single 61 Mb chromosome at 100 bp was killed for memory, and the whole mouse genome at 100 bp now takes three minutes and 2.3 GB.

  • An aggregate peak analysis is now coolpuppy’s pileup wherever a window reaches the diagonal, and its observed / expected normalisation is coolpuppy’s (§18.8). A loop window that crossed the diagonal took the cells below it from the map read whole — contacts it already held above, mirrored — where coolpuppy cuts from the upper triangle and leaves them out: on 4DN’s mouse Hi-C at 10 kb, with chromosight’s 2,515 loops, the two pileups were up to ten per cent apart in the cells nearest the diagonal. Those cells are left out now, and so are the main diagonal and the one beside it, as coolpuppy leaves them out by default — diagonals left out, a new setting, 2 to begin with and 0 for every diagonal. A pileup on the diagonal keeps its whole window but for those diagonals, which is coolpuppy’s local pileup, and its P2LL — whose centre there is mostly the diagonal — falls with them: from 60 to 15 over the yeast demo’s ATAC peaks. Observed / expected was wrong off the diagonal: it divided the finished pileup by an expected looked up at each cell’s distance from the pileup’s centre, taken from the windows’ own cells, and put the centre of that loop set at 0.2 where coolpuppy puts it at 2.9. Each window is now divided, cell by cell, by its own sequence’s expected at the cell’s own diagonal — cooltools’ balanced.avg, read as a band as deep as the farthest window — before the mean. That is the pileup’s normalisation alone: the map’s own O/E (§7.5) is computed as it was. Raw, over the expected and on the diagonal, every cell agrees with coolpuppy’s to 5 × 10⁻⁶, the same cells left as gaps; a new golden, apa_diagonal, holds the task’s own runner to it on the demo map’s loops, the 55 of 200 that reach the diagonal included.

  • A track named to chromosomix is no longer read whole to learn what it is (§23.1). Every file under --tracks was read into memory to look at its first four bytes before a bigWig or a .2bit was opened by range: a second a gigabyte before any run began, and a refusal for any file past 2 GiB, Node’s ceiling for one read. Four bytes are read now. A metaplot over a 957 MB ATAC-seq bigWig went from 15 s to 11, with the same numbers.

  • Signing in to Dropbox opens the Files column on it (§24.9). The folder list hears about a bucket from the form that saves it and about a Drive folder from the pane that adds it, and nothing told it about a Dropbox sign-in — so the Files column, which exists only while a place is connected, never appeared: browse my Dropbox closed the dialog on the landing page with no error, and a sign-out from the dialog left a dead row behind. The sign-in and the sign-out are now passed on (browse/dropbox.ts). An expiry is not: the click that finds one says sign in to Dropbox in the column, and the column stays to show it. The first live sign-in, on chromosomix.app, found this.

  • Desktop: bigWig lanes draw (§15). The bigWig reader fetches its own inflate wasm from a data: URL inside the annotations worker, and the desktop’s policy, which reaches its workers, refused it — inside the worker, so nothing in the page said so — and every bigWig lane stayed on fetching…. connect-src admits data:, which has no server behind it, and the site’s policy stays its mirror.

  • A workspace, link, ?config=, embed message or saved tab naming an unknown view or transform opens (§5.5, §5.6, §21.1). The address was read by an allow-list and everything else was not: a state arriving as an object — a workspace, a share link, a ?config= file, an embed’s navigate, the durable record of a tab — was cast as it came, so view: 'banana' sat on Opening… for ever and transform: 'banana' threw on the first paint. Every one of them is now read by the hash’s own reader, which keeps what it knows and drops the rest; the twelve demo workspaces restore key for key.

  • A read stored without its bases (*) shows no mismatches it does not have (§8.13). The mismatch walk compared the bytes after an empty sequence — its tags — with the reference as though they were bases; it now walks an aligned operation only as far as the read has bases.

  • chromosomix reads its command line by what each command takes (§23.1). A flag took the word after it as its value unless that word began with --, so coverage a.cool --no-balance b.hic ran over a.cool alone with exit 0, coverage --quiet a.cool refused itself for want of a file, and --out --quiet wrote into a directory called true. Choices are checked against the task’s options, switches and numbers against what they can be, and what a replayed workspace recorded the same way — exit 2, with the values there are.

The PDF page let go of a path at the first operator that painted it, where a canvas keeps it until beginPath, so a shape filled and then stroked printed as its fill and a stroke of nothing: every exported box plot lost its outline, whiskers and median, the reader’s arrowheads came out as white halos with nothing inside, and a bar track’s outline and the 1D browser’s chips lost their borders.   • The palette’s door survives a half-width window. Below 60 rem the search bar was hidden outright, which left ⌘P — and every command with no button of its own — with nothing on screen to name it. It keeps its badge and loses its sentence.

  • 3 problem(s) is 3 problems, and a validation that is running says running.

  • The whole metagenome no longer dies with Array buffer allocation failed at bin level (§7.3). The bin-level block was summed into a full-square Float64 and copied to Float32, about 200 MB at its peak against the contig level’s 134 MB for the same raster, so on a tab short of memory the default level failed where the others worked. It now sums the upper triangle and finishes straight into Float32, which is what the other levels cost. And if the tab still cannot allocate it, the view falls back to contig × contig (a cooler’s chromosome × chromosome), starts that read without asking again, and greys out bin × bin with a line saying why.

A MAG-QC table joined into a metacool brings its own Length and Contigs, so the header read name length contigs bins Length GC-content … — identical numbers under two capitalisations, with no way to tell which one a sort was using. Security

Section titled “Security”

  • Every web build carries a Content-Security-Policy (§11, §21, §22). The site, each pinned build and the package write one policy into index.html as a <meta>: scripts and styles from the page’s own origin and Google sign-in’s documented sources, no inline script and no style=, connections to https:, the loopback and data:, and no frame-ancestors, so an embed stays frameable. The build fails if its page holds anything the policy would refuse. A script injected through a name in a file would have run with the reader’s S3 key in reach; it would be refused now.

  • The single file carries a policy computed over itself (§19). It names the hash of each inline script and stylesheet, and reaches no host: connect-src admits only data: — where the bigWig reader keeps its own wasm — and the blob workers inherit the policy.

  • A chart’s tooltip shows a file’s names as text (§18.13). A sequence, sample or series name was written into the tooltip as HTML, which the chart library parses on every hover, so a file with a sequence called <img src=x onerror=…> ran its script when the pointer crossed a Cis/trans or track-correlation cell. Every formatter now escapes what it prints, through one helper — the names, the numbers and a series’ colour, which a workspace file carries as surely as a name.

  • A binary file is held to its own size, whatever its counts claim (§10, §12). A .hic, a BAM’s BAI or CSI, a .2bit and a binary g3d with its MessagePack header were read on the strength of the counts and lengths they declare, so a truncated download, a flipped bit or a hostile file sized a typed array, a read or a loop: a sixteen-byte .hic block declaring fifty million records allocated 572 MiB, one negative vector length in a footer read 341 MiB of a 256 MiB file, and a twenty-four-byte .csi declaring a depth near a million would hold the annotations worker for some twenty minutes a query. Each count is now refused — naming the format, the field and the value — unless it is a count, under the format’s own maximum and small enough for the bytes left to hold it, and an offset and length must name bytes inside the file. Over 1,140 seeded corruptions of files the repository’s own writers produce, no reader reads more than four times its file, makes an array past 30 KB or takes a tenth of a second.

  • Every inflate stops at a cap (§10, §12). A .hic block, a g3d model, a gzipped or bgzipped text file, a BGZF span, a .pairs stream, a whole file the command line keeps and a share link were each inflated to the end before anything looked at the size. The decompressor is now fed 64 KiB at a time and stopped as soon as its output passes a cap in proportion to what went in — sixty-four times for a .hic or g3d block, where straw allows ten — a BGZF member is held to the format’s own 64 KiB and text to 2^29 bytes, and a line of a plain-gzip .pairs past 64 MiB is refused.
