Reading a contact map

The line under a map’s heading gives the block’s size and what one cell is worth: 1,532 × 1,532 bins · effective resolution: 1 kb.

The Map card, in the Aesthetics panel, sets how the map is drawn, from top to bottom: picture (undo every pan and zoom, the 45° turn, the diagonal lock, O/E), compare with a second file, score, smooth, transform, colours, clip and overlays. All but the overlays and the diagonal lock are in the link.

Resolution

Section titled “Resolution”

At the far end of the bar above the map is the resolution, in one row:

  • refine on zoom, ticked by default: the map is read at the resolution of the screen, and finer as you zoom, down to the finest the file stores. While it is ticked the rest of the row is greyed and follows the map, so it says which resolution is drawn; where the map is drawn at a size the file does not store, the slider’s thumb sits on the nearest slot and the box names that size.
  • a slider with a round slot for each bin size the file stores, coarsest on the left, the two ends named; point at a slot for its size. Untick refine on zoom, then click a slot, drag along the slider, or use the arrow keys on it; - and + step one coarser or finer.
  • a box naming the resolution; click it for a list of every bin size.
  • a +, which opens Add a resolution.

With refine on zoom unticked, the resolution you pick is what is drawn, read where you are, without leaving the window you zoomed to. Unticking keeps the resolution on screen as your pick, and leaves refine on zoom off for the maps you open next; ticking it gives the resolution back to the zoom. The link carries the pick as resolution=, and a link that names one opens at it, with refine on zoom unticked, whatever the recipient’s own setting. It stays as you go to another chromosome, and goes when you open another file. A window too wide for the resolution picked (8,192 bins a side) is drawn from the finest stored resolution that fits, and the summary line says so: 10 kb is too fine for this window: zoom in (Resolution and the zoom).

Add a resolution takes a whole multiple of one of the file’s bin sizes, such as 3 kb on 1 kb bins, summed from the stored bins. It shows as 3 kb*, its slot ringed with dots, and a file takes up to twelve; while it is the one picked, the × beside the box removes it, and the map goes to the stored resolution it was summed from. Counts are exact, but balanced values are an estimate, since cooler zoomify --balance balances each resolution on its own. A link to it opens at it anywhere, added or not.

A task runs at the resolution you picked, or, with none picked, at the one the file was opened at, whatever the screen shows; each task’s own Resolution row, first in its form, can set another for that task alone (Analyses). On the whole genome, level, at the head of the Sequences card (General panel), switches between bin × bin and chromosome × chromosome.

What the cells hold

Section titled “What the cells hold”

score sets what the cells hold:

Score Each cell holds
counts the contacts stored for that pair of bins
balanced the counts with each bin’s bias divided out by the file’s weights, when it has them
VC, VC √, ICE a normalisation computed from the block on screen
expected what the cell’s distance from the diagonal predicts: the baseline of an O/E
arm decay that baseline along both arms of a circular sequence
correlation the correlation between rows of the balanced O/E, on a file with weights
a column’s name another column of the file’s own pixel table

A file with weights opens balanced on log10; one without, counts on log1p.

Balanced or raw counts

Section titled “Balanced or raw counts”

For balanced, a cooler’s weights are those cooler balance wrote, applied as cooler applies them; a .hic brings its own normalisation (SCALE, else KR, else the coverage ones), which the normalisation row of the File card (General panel) names. A bin the balancer dropped has its row and column drawn as paper, not as zero.

VC, VC √ and ICE normalise a file without weights, or another way; on a window they are the window’s normalisation, not the chromosome’s. Each agrees with cooler and cooltools cell for cell, to the map’s precision (How the results are checked). Juicer’s KR is not offered: it reaches the same weights as ICE.

Expected and correlation

Section titled “Expected and correlation”

Far from the diagonal, expected averages few cells, so check it before trusting an O/E there. O/E is greyed while it is drawn.

correlation, the picture compartments are read from, is drawn for square blocks of up to 4,096 bins a side. The Compartments task uses the same arithmetic.

The colour scale

Section titled “The colour scale”

transform maps a value onto the ramp: log1p for counts, log10 for balanced values, log2 for ratios (twice and half equally far from no change), or none, on which little but the diagonal shows.

clip sets where the ramp starts and where it saturates, in the transform’s units, as its label says (clip (log10)); the line under it gives the range in the readout’s units, auto · 0.000066 – 0.089 balanced. Type a bound to fix it; a change of transform puts it back to automatic. The automatic range is measured on the block that was read, and kept as you zoom into it, so the colours hold still: from 0 to the 99th percentile of the non-zero counts, or for balanced values from the 1st to the 99th percentile of the cells that hold a contact. ⇅ is always there, and measures the range again on what is on screen, by the same rule — after a zoom from a whole chromosome into a few domains, the range of those domains.

The key, in the map’s top-right corner, names the score and the transform, gives one cell’s size and shows the two bounds: click either to type it, and empty it for automatic. legend, under overlays, hides it.

colours picks the ramp; a ✓ marks those safe with a colour-vision deficiency.

Ramp
default white through yellow and red to black: what a map opens on
afmhot_r, afmhot matplotlib’s ramps of those names
Reds white, then red to a dark maroon
cividis ✓, viridis ✓, magma ✓ even in lightness
RdBu_r diverging, red for more, blue for less: what a ratio or an O/E opens on
BuWtRd diverging, with more contrast, white at its middle
seismic diverging: chromosight’s pileup ramp

Observed over expected

Section titled “Observed over expected”

O/E, in the picture row, or e, divides each cell by the mean over every pair of bins as far apart in this block, with one value for each pair of different sequences. It works on any score, balanced counts most often.

It switches the map to log2, a range symmetric about 1 and RdBu_r: red for more contacts than expected, blue for fewer. Switching it off restores your transform, colours and clip. A cell where nothing was observed is drawn as paper, not as the strongest depletion.

On a sequence declared circular, or whose origin has been set, two buttons join O/E. arms also divides out the decay along the secondary diagonal through the origin, leaving a bacterial chromosome’s compartments. ori, on a circle, frames the sequence with its origin in the middle (press again for its first base). Declare circles and origins in the Chromosomes table (Circular sequences).

Smoothing

Section titled “Smoothing”

smooth averages each cell with its neighbours: 3 × 3, 5 × 5, 7 × 7, or adaptive, whose window grows until it holds enough contacts (serpentine’s criterion, as a sliding window). It turns the O/E on. Smoothing comes before the O/E and the ratio of two files, so a zero cell among contacts is no longer a hole. Past 16 million cells, adaptive falls back to 3 × 3, and the note under the heading says so.

Drawing maps to one total

Section titled “Drawing maps to one total”

A deeper library is brighter everywhere. The contacts box, after resolution at the far end of the bar, draws every map in the tab to one total before it is shown or analysed: type 10M, 23,000,000, 2.5e7 or 500k and press Enter, or empty it to read the files as stored. Its tooltip gives each map’s own total.

A deeper map is subsampled to exactly that total, as cooltools random-sample --count N --exact does; a shallower one is oversampled through Boost-HiC, with new contacts drawn from a probability map built from it. An oversample is an inference, and wherever the map is named it says which was done.

The draw has a fixed seed, so one total is one map in every view, after a reload and on the command line (--contacts). Balanced values are rescaled with it, so a subsampled map is not drawn fainter. Every task reads the drawn map, and a saved figure’s caption says so.

With two maps or more (a deck, or a second file in the lower triangle), two chevrons after the box type the shallowest or the deepest map’s total. The link carries the total as contacts=, and it stays with the file across sequences.

Caution

Some maps cannot be drawn to a total, and say why, with read as stored to go back: counts that are not whole numbers, a file storing every contact twice, an .scool, a value column other than counts and, to oversample, a sequence over 3,000 bins (use a coarser resolution).

What is drawn over the map

Section titled “What is drawn over the map”

overlays switches dividers between sequences (d), breaks, the same boundaries as ticks outside the plot (m), block labels (l), base-pair ticks (k), the legend, the crosshair (x) and the tooltip card (p). This browser keeps them; a link does not.

Hovering a cell

Section titled “Hovering a cell”

Point at a cell and a card reads its positions, counted from 1, their distance and its value. On an O/E map it adds the count the ratio was read from, which says whether to believe it; with a second file, both files’ counts. The status line at the foot of the window keeps the reading; tooltip (p) hides the card. Shift snaps the reading onto the diagonal.

Right-click a cell for:

  • Copy this: the cells alone, as a PNG with no axis, key or frame;
  • Mark this point: a Vertical line, Horizontal line, Crosshair or Arrowhead at the cell, added to the Manual annotations card;
  • Copy coordinates: the cell’s two positions, counted from 1, without separators.
