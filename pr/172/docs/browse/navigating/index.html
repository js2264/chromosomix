Moving around

Dragging and scrolling move the picture, and leave the address alone; the map reads what they bring into view as you go, without stopping (Resolution and the zoom). The locus box, the zoom steps, the crumbs and the sequence keys change the range: they read a new block, the address follows, and the back button undoes them.

Pan and zoom

Section titled “Pan and zoom”
  • Drag, or one finger on a touch screen, to pan; a tap opens what it lands on.
  • Scroll up or down, or pinch, to zoom about the pointer. With the pointer on the diagonal, or within four pixels of it, the zoom is about the diagonal, so the view stays on it.
  • Scroll sideways to slide along the map’s columns. On a Mac, hold ⌘ and two fingers move it in any direction.
  • Double-click (a double tap), 0, or the first button of the picture row on the Map card (in the Aesthetics panel) undoes every pan and zoom: the whole sequence, range or genome you opened, at the resolution that fits the screen, however far in you had zoomed.

Panning and zooming reach as far as the sequence the range is on, or the genome on the whole genome: near the edge of what was read, the map reads on ahead of you. To see another sequence, change the range.

Zoom into a region

Section titled “Zoom into a region”
  • ⌥ Option-drag (Alt on Windows and Linux) a box: on the diagonal it opens as a window, off it as the block between its two stretches. Add Shift to keep it square on the diagonal.
  • Shift-drag along either axis to zoom the picture to that stretch, on the diagonal. While Shift is down, the readout and the crosshair snap to the diagonal.
  • Both copy what they framed, counted from 1 without separators: chrIV:500001-510000, or chrI:1-10000|chrII:40001-50000 for a box, rows then columns.
  • A click on a window of one sequence opens all of it. On the whole genome, a click opens the chromosome under the pointer or, off the diagonal, the block between two.

Stay on the diagonal

Section titled “Stay on the diagonal”

Space, or the diagonal button in the picture row, slides the view onto the diagonal and keeps every drag, scroll, arrow key and ⌥ Option-drag box on it until you press again. The lock holds from one sequence to the next and on every map of a deck, and this browser remembers it. It is greyed on a block the diagonal does not cross, and on a turned map.

Type a place

Section titled “Type a place”

The box beside the heading shows the window as IGV, UCSC and papers print it, IV:1,000,001-1,400,000: counted from 1, both ends included, so a coordinate copied from another browser lands on the same bases. Type a place and press Enter or go; / puts the caret in the box.

Type To open
IV the whole sequence
IV:1,000,001-1,400,000 that window; commas optional, and IV:1.2Mb-1.4Mb, an en dash and Ensembl’s IV:1000001..1400000 are read too
I:1-200,000 × II:1-200,000 the block between two windows, rows then columns
a gene’s name or accession that gene, from the starred feature set (below)
a band, such as Xq28 that band, from a cytoBand you have loaded

Between two windows, an x with a space either side, a |, or a space before the second name works as well as ×. A sequence may be spelt as another tool spells it (chr1 for a file that says 1); a name the file does not have is refused, with the names it has.

Go to a gene

Section titled “Go to a gene”

Load a gene model, or any named features, and the box searches it as you type, by name or accession: up to ten matches, an exact name first. Picking one frames the gene with a quarter of its length either side. The box searches the set whose star is filled on the Tracks card; press another row’s star to switch.

A sequence’s name still opens the sequence: it heads the list, above the genes that begin with it, and Enter opens it.

Go to another sequence

Section titled “Go to another sequence”
  • > and < (. and , on a QWERTY keyboard), or the ‹ › buttons beside the title, open the next and the previous sequence in the file’s order.
  • In the Chromosomes table, from the crumb after the file’s name or c, click a row.
  • On the Sequences card, in the General panel, click a name for the whole sequence, or tick several to draw them against each other; show the whole genome clears the ticks.
  • In the Whole genome, from the arrow beside the Chromosomes crumb or w, click a cell. Where it is a large read, it waits for a button that says what it will read.
  • The Genome minimap, in the General panel, marks the view on every sequence laid end to end: click a sequence’s band to open it, or drag across the strip for a window.

Zoom and step by range

Section titled “Zoom and step by range”

The buttons beside the box, 10× and 3× out and 3× and 10× in, scale the window about its centre and read it again, from one bin up to the whole sequence. The two arrows after go move the window half its width upstream or downstream. On a block between two sequences they are all greyed.

Resolution and the zoom

Section titled “Resolution and the zoom”

With refine on zoom ticked, at the head of the resolution row (Resolution), and no resolution picked, a map opens at about the size of the screen: a whole chromosome is read from a coarser resolution the file stores, a cell or so to each pixel, which is what puts it on screen in a fraction of a second. It is always one the file stores, the nearest to a pixel: the slider’s thumb sits on a slot. The whole genome (W) opens the same way, whichever resolution the link opened the file at. As you zoom in, the finer data is read for the stretch you are looking at, down to the finest resolution the file stores, whichever it was opened at: while you go on zooming in, nothing is read, and the moment you pause, the stretch you paused on is. The map never stops following the scroll or the drag while it reads: for a moment its cells are larger and a small spinner turns in its top right corner, then it sharpens where you are. The slider follows, greyed, its thumb moving from slot to slot. The line under the heading says what one cell is worth at any moment, 785 × 785 bins · effective resolution: 250 kb on a whole chromosome. As you pan, the stretch ahead of the edge of what was read is read before you reach it.

Untick it to pick the resolution yourself — a slot, the box, - or + — and the map is drawn at it wherever you zoom; unticking keeps the resolution on screen as your pick, so nothing jumps. A pick is read where you are, keeping the window you zoomed to, and goes in the link, so a link opens at it, with refine on zoom unticked. A window too wide for that resolution, 8,192 bins a side, is drawn coarser, and the line under the heading says so — 10 kb is too fine for this window: zoom in — until you zoom in far enough for it. Inside what was read, panning and zooming read nothing more; past its edge, the map reads on. Tick refine on zoom to hand the resolution back to the zoom; maps you open while it is unticked are drawn at the resolution they were opened at until you pick another. Refine on zoom is also in the command palette (⌘ P or Ctrl P), where it does what the tick does; it applies to every map, and this browser remembers it.

A figure is the map as it is on screen, at the resolution it is drawn at (Figures): zoom in, or pick a resolution, for a finer one. An analysis runs at the resolution you picked, or at the one the file was opened at, never at the screen’s (Analyses).

A block of several million cells — a whole chromosome at a fine resolution picked — is first drawn at the size of the screen, which is what makes it appear quickly. Its finer detail is drawn when you first zoom in: for a moment after that zoom the picture is softer, then it sharpens. Nothing is read again, since the block is already in hand.

And with refine on zoom off and no resolution picked, while such a block is still being read, the window is shown from a coarser resolution the file stores, when it has one: clicking a whole chromosome puts a coarse map on screen in a fraction of a second, with a spinner in its corner, and the map at the file’s resolution replaces it when it arrives. The coarse map’s key says its own resolution. Its cells are sums of many finer ones, so its compartments look stronger than they will in the finer map. You can scroll, drag and hover it straight away: the finer map lands on the stretch you have moved to, or fills the panel if you have not moved. Saving or copying the figure, and running an analysis, wait for the finer map, and the Export card says so. A window that is already about as coarse as that is read once, as before.

Crumbs, back and forward

Section titled “Crumbs, back and forward”

Click a crumb in the trail above the view to go back to it: the file’s name (which opens another file), the Chromosomes table (its arrow switches to the Whole genome), the sequence, the window. The far end of the bar holds the map’s resolution and contacts (Reading a contact map).

Go back and Go forward, in the window’s header, are the browser’s back and forward; the chevron beside Go back lists up to twenty earlier steps, nearest first, to jump to one.

A step is a crumb, a typed locus, a zoom or pan button, or a change of sequence; a drag, a scroll or a pinch is not. So a link you copy opens the window you opened, not wherever you panned. In the genome browser, the address (locus=) follows every move without adding steps.

From the keyboard

Section titled “From the keyboard”

Besides the keys above, - and + step one resolution coarser or finer (with refine on zoom unticked), b bookmarks the view, and ? lists every shortcut. Once you have clicked the map, the arrow keys pan, Page Up and Page Down zoom about the centre, and Home refits. Once you have clicked the genome browser’s tracks, the arrow keys pan, - and + zoom by three like the 3× buttons, and 0 shows the whole genome. The full list is in Keyboard and command palette.
