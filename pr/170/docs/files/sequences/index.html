Reference sequences

Beside a map, a FASTA or a .2bit draws a lane of GC content; in the 1D browser, the bases once each has a pixel, and the six reading frames on request (The genome browser). With no map, it opens the 1D browser over its genome. It feeds the sequence tasks, such as Find a motif, Base composition and Restriction sites, and colours a BAM’s mismatches.

FASTA

Section titled “FASTA”

A FASTA (.fa, .fasta, .fna or .fas) under 64 MB is read whole. A larger one needs its .fai, beside it or named with #index=<address>, to be read by range; without one it is read whole up to 300 MB, and refused above with the command that indexes it:

Read by range, a sequence’s GC lane draws nothing over a window wider than 4 Mb (GC under 4 Mb, letters at base resolution — zoom in), Origin and terminus declines it, and a BAM’s mismatches are coloured only over the stretch the sequence lane last drew.

A gzipped or bgzipped FASTA is always read whole, and refused past 300 MB, since its .gzi index is not read: decompress and index it, or use a .2bit.

.2bit

Section titled “.2bit”

A .2bit is read by range, with an indexed FASTA’s limits, and holds its own index: one file, a quarter of the FASTA’s size. Gaps are read as runs of N; soft-masking is not shown. Refused: a version-1 .2bit, made for files over 4 GB (convert it with twoBitToFa and faToTwoBit); one declaring more than 100,000 sequences; and a truncated one.

One genome, many names

Section titled “One genome, many names”

Wherever a name is asked for — a track, a second map, a 3D model, the locus box — the file’s own spelling is tried first, then these rules:

Rule Example
another case Chr1 = chr1
the same accession, undecorated ref|NC_001133| = NC_001133
the chr prefix chr1 = chrom1 = chromosome_1 = 1
leading zeros 01 = 1
Roman numerals chrXII = 12
an organelle spelled another way chrM = MT = MtDNA = NC_012920.1
the accession NC_000001.11 = chr1; chrUn_GL000220v1 = GL000220.1
another version of the accession NC_000001.10 ≈ NC_000001.11 — offered, and flagged

They never quietly match two different sequences:

  • The whole list decides. X is chromosome ten in a list with IX and XI (budding yeast), and the sex chromosome where the numerals stop at V (C. elegans) or where chromosomes are numbered both ways (a host with a yeast spike-in). Bare numbers in a list longer than any genome’s chromosomes, such as an assembler’s contigs, match only as spelled.
  • Not equated: an arm and its chromosome (2L is not 2); a patch or one parent’s copy and the chromosome (_random, _alt, _fix, _hap1, _maternal); a linkage group (LG) and a chromosome number. A name that follows no convention matches only exactly.
  • Lengths check the match. Sequences matched across conventions with different lengths, as two assembly versions have, are flagged: Matched across two naming conventions, but the sequences are different lengths….

A map in a deck notes under its heading how it read a name (“chrIV” read as “IV”, by the chr prefix). On a track’s row, No sequence in this file matches the map (0 of 16) means every convention failed: the file is likely about another genome.

Circular sequences

Section titled “Circular sequences”

A bacterial chromosome, a plasmid or a mitochondrion arrives linear, the contacts joining its ends in the map’s far corner, until it is declared circular.

Tick it in the circular column of the Chromosomes table, or of a genome opened from a FASTA or tracks. Its origin, beside the tick, is its first base until you type a 1-based position or let Origin and terminus place it; the task never overwrites a position you typed.

On a file’s first open, its 50 longest sequences with at least 50 bins and no more than 10 Mb are scored on their contacts across the two ends against the diagonal’s at the same separation: about 1 for a circle, 0 for a line. At 0.2 or more a sequence is ticked, with a banner saying where to untick it and its score in the tick’s tooltip; an untick stays. A small circle at a coarse resolution may be missed: tick it yourself. A sequence over 10 Mb is not scored: nearly every circular chromosome is shorter (a bacterium’s is about 4 Mb at the median, and the large ones of Myxococcus and Bradyrhizobium about 9), so a human or mouse chromosome, or a chicken macrochromosome, is never read for it. Tick the rare longer circle, such as a Sorangium chromosome, yourself.

Once ticked:

  • a window can cross the junction: chrom:2,900,000-100,000 on a 3 Mb circle is the 200 kb across it;
  • the map offers ori, the whole circle with its origin in the middle, and arms, observed over expected along both arms rather than the diagonal alone (Reading a contact map);
  • the analyses measure separation the short way round: P(s), insulation, the directionality index, the HiC score, the scalogram, compartments and the tasks that read from the origin. Chromosight and the observed-over-expected map do not.

Declarations are kept with the file and in a saved workspace, not in a link. On the command line, chromosomix circular <map> runs the same test, and --circular <name> and --origin <name:pos> declare them for a run (Command line).
