This is a preview of pull request #170, for its review: it is replaced on every push and taken down when the pull request closes. The published documentation is at doc.chromosomix.app.

Unreleased

What has changed on main since the latest release, and will be in the next one.

Added

Section titled “Added”
  • Every task that reads a map has a Resolution row, the first of its form (§18.4). A list of every rung of the map’s ladder, stored and added (marked *), starting on the one picked in the crumb row or, with none picked, the one the file was opened at; every setting after it that counts in bins counts in its bins. A choice there is that task’s alone, on this file: the task is framed, priced and read at that rung, from its own entry of the file, and the crumb row and every other task stay where they were — so compartments can run at 250 kb and insulation at 10 kb without the map moving. Choosing the rung the task would read anyway clears the choice, and another file forgets it. A task that reads no map, a starred companion of a deck, an import and a ladder with nothing to choose have no such row.

Changed

Section titled “Changed”

  • A map opens at the screen’s resolution and sharpens as you zoom, down to the finest resolution the file stores: Refine on zoom is on by default (§2, §7.2, §12). With nothing picked, a view’s first read is the stored rung nearest a cell to each pixel of the plot, so a chromosome opens quickly whatever resolution the file was opened at — chicken chr1 at 10 kb in 0.34 s, 785 cells of its 250 kb rung in one 2.1 MB request, where it took 1.9 s; mouse chr1 at 10 kb in 1.7 s where it took 4.8 — and every zoom reads the next grid, down to the finest rung the file stores rather than the one it was opened at: a zoom into chicken chr1 opened at 10 kb walks down the rungs the file stores from 250 kb, on past 10 kb to its 5, 2 and 1 kb. Every refine read is screen-sized too. A refine used to read as much as memory allowed: the same zoom read a 15,027-bin square (226 M cells) at its second wheel notch. Only a file with one resolution, a map beside a second file and a read too wide for the coarsest rung are pooled, and a pooled read is never more than the reader’s 4,096 cells a side: on a plot wider than about 1,280 pixels it was asked for more, and the reader pooled it coarser than asked.

  • The resolution is a row of its own: refine on zoom, a slider, a box and the + (§2, §3.4, §5.5). At the far end of the crumb bar: the refine on zoom tick, ticked by default; a slider of fixed width with a round slot for each rung the file stores, coarsest on the left, written out only at its two ends and naming a slot under the pointer; a box naming the resolution, a menu of every rung when pressed, with no arrow; and the + that adds one. Ticked, the slider, the box and the + are greyed and follow the map, the thumb gliding from slot to slot as a zoom sharpens it, and sitting on the nearest slot where the grid drawn is one no rung stores, which the box then names. Unticked, you pick: a slot, a drag along the slider from anywhere on it, the thumb included, the box, the arrow keys on the slider, which keep the keyboard from one rung to the next, or − and +, which are greyed while the zoom picks. Unticking keeps the resolution on screen as the pick, and so does the palette’s Refine on zoom, which does exactly what the tick does; ticking hands the resolution back to the zoom. It replaces the row of labelled stops and the dropdown a ladder past nine rungs became; a resolution you added is a dotted slot, and its × sits beside the box while it is picked.

  • A resolution picked is one key in the link, resolution=, and it wins over Refine on zoom (§2, §3.4, §5.5). A rung the file stores and one you added are both resolution=, in bp, and the recipient’s file resolves it — the stored rung of that size, else the coarsest stored rung that divides it — so a link naming a resolution its sender added draws it on a machine that never added it; a value no rung reaches, or past 100 Mb, is ignored. coarsen=, the key an added resolution had, is no longer written, and is read as resolution= in an old link, a stored tab or session and a widget’s arguments. A map whose link names a resolution is drawn at it whatever the preference says, and its row shows the tick off, live rather than greyed; a pick changes the link, never the preference. It goes with you to another chromosome, a table, the minimap and < and >, as the cells and the contacts total do, and is dropped with the file.

  • The resolution you pick is the picture, read where you are (§2, §3.4, §7.2). A pick is drawn at every zoom, read around the view as far as a block at it holds: 8,192 bins a side for a rung the file stores, 4,096 cells for one you added. A window too wide for that is drawn from the finest stored rung no finer than the pick that fits, or from an added resolution’s own cells summed further, and the line under the map says so — 10 kb is too fine for this window: zoom in — until the window fits. A rung finer than the one the file was opened at is read as any other: 1 kb picked on chicken chr1 opened at 10 kb draws 1 kb once the window is under about 8 Mb. A pick is read in place, keeping the window you zoomed to, in the region, genome, pair and whole-genome views, where it re-opened the file at the whole sequence, and its block is measured for a colour range of its own, as a map opened at it was. With refine on zoom off and nothing picked, the rung the file was opened at is read the same way. Either way a pan to the edge of what was read, or a zoom out past it, reads on, where it used to stop there.

  • A map is only ever drawn at a resolution the file stores (§2, §7.2). A screen-sized read takes the stored rung nearest a cell to each pixel, where it took the coarsest rung no bigger than a pixel and summed it to the pixel: chr6 of the K562 .mcool opened at 1 Mb was its 100 kb rung summed two by two into 200 kb cells, every 100 kb pixel downloaded, 56 MB; it is now its 250 kb rung, 35 MB. A read as memory allows takes the finest stored rung no finer than the one asked for that fits, where a gap in the ladder had the reader sum the open map into a grid nobody stored. The resolutions you add with + are still summed, as declared.

  • The whole-genome view opens at the screen’s resolution, whichever entry the link names (§2, §7.2, §9). Its first read was planned from the entry the file was opened at: the K562 .mcool was drawn at 1 Mb, 3,102 bins and 17 MB, from its 1 Mb entry, and at 500 kb, 6,188 bins and 57 MB (54 s from a machine far from the bucket), from its 25 kb one. With refine on zoom ticked and nothing picked it is now its 5 Mb rung from either, 631 bins and 3.9 or 5.8 MB, and sharpens as you zoom; a pick is read as a region view reads it, and with the preference off so is the open map. A coarser stored rung read instead of the open one’s pixels is not asked about first; a pick finer than the open rung is, by the open rung’s pixel count. The question’s open at … instead picks that rung where you are rather than re-opening the file.

  • A block read bin for bin is at most 8,192 bins a side (§2, §7.2). It was 16,384 on any desktop: a block of 268 million cells. On a zoom out from 6 Mb of chicken chr1 at a picked 1 kb, the read around the view took 3.6 s and took the tab from 0.9 to 2.0 GB; it takes 1.0 s and leaves it at 1.3. A rung picked is drawn whole over up to 8,192 of its bins.

  • An analysis runs at the resolution picked, else at the one the file was opened at — never the screen’s (§18.4). A rung picked in the crumb row is read from its own entry of the file — sample.mcool::resolutions/25000 while the file is open at 10 kb — and one you added is summed from the rung it is made of. While the zoom picks, a task reads the rung the file was opened at, so the same link and the same run give the same numbers at any zoom and on any screen. The drawer’s heading, the provenance line, the command it writes and the run’s label name it, and follow a pick as it happens: a pick read in place leaves the file open where it was, and a task follows the pick rather than the entry. An import’s tasks still read its open map.

  • A bookmark is saved at the resolution asked for, and going to one picks it (§8.10). The resolution picked, else the rung the file was opened at — never the one the zoom has drawn — and arriving at the bookmark puts it in the link, where it re-opened the file at that rung’s entry. A bookmark at the rung opened, gone to while nothing is picked, leaves the zoom picking.

  • The spinner sits in the map’s top right corner, left of its legend (§2, §7.2). It turned on the canvas, in the plot’s bottom-left corner over the first label of each axis — 0 bp on a whole chromosome — just when a preview that spins while the reader zooms it has those labels read. It is now a small mark beside the key, and only for a read that takes longer than 140 ms, so a quick refine shows nothing.

  • The colour range’s ⇅ is always there, and measures the view (§7.1). It was greyed while the range was automatic, but a range kept across a zoom is no longer the view’s own: after a zoom from a whole chromosome to a few domains, it was the chromosome’s. A press now measures the stretch of the block in view, and the next refine keeps that. A block coarser than the one the range came from is measured again, as a wider one was. A lane’s limits, which share the control, no longer grey it either.

  • The tutorial unticks refine on zoom before it picks 250 kb (§30.3), in a step of its own: fifty-nine steps, where it was fifty-eight.

  • Zooming never freezes the map (§2). A refine read used to hold the map still, refusing every gesture for the seconds it took; now the block on screen keeps every pan and zoom while the next one is read, a spinner turns in its corner, and the sharper block lands wherever you have got to. A zoom that outruns its read calls it off, and a zoom going inward reads where it pauses (below), its next step early; a pan reads ahead of the edge of its block, and the last step onto the finest rung is always taken. Sixteen wheel notches into chicken chr1 at 10 kb froze the map for 2.0 s and stalled the page 1.9 s at once; they now stall it 0.18 s at most. The freeze and the Escape that ended it are gone, and a frame of a gesture no longer forces a layout to measure the plot.

  • A zoom going inward reads where it pauses, and a read called off stops (§2, §12). While the view keeps shrinking — a wheel’s notches come tens of milliseconds apart, a trackpad’s frames sixteen — nothing is read; once it has not shrunk for 150 ms, the window it paused on is. The block on screen covers the view throughout, so nothing goes missing meanwhile: it is only coarser for longer. A pan, a zoom outward and a jump to a bookmark or a typed window read at once. The zoom called off a read it had outrun, but only a pooled read looked for the cancel: a dense read — every read at a rung the file stores, so nearly every read a zoom makes — ran to its last window, and the read the zoom was waiting for queued behind it, the worker reading one request at a time. On the 4DN map of K562 from S3, a wheel zoom from the whole of chr5 to 20 Mb fired the 100 kb rung’s read a fifth of a second in and called it off half a second later, and that read went on for 28 MB and about 7 s more. A dense read now stops at the end of the window it is in, and a window is 250,000 pixels where it was a million, since a window is as long as a cancel waits; a read that opening a chromosome calls off stops too, and the same picture of chr5 comes in 5.3 s and 25 MB where it took 5.5 s and 34 MB. But a read stopped at once has still walked its rung’s chunk index from cold — that 100 kb read held the worker 3.4 s and 13 requests — and the read the pause never starts costs nothing. In medians of three runs, from a fresh session in a 1600 × 1000 window with wheel notches 50 ms apart, the zoom from chr5 opened at 250 kb to a 20 Mb window, landing on the 25 kb rung, takes 5.8 s, 20.7 MB and 16 requests where it took 11.7 s, 65 MB and 52, its block 1,612 bins read for the window the zoom paused on rather than 1,968 for one it passed at 24.6 Mb; on to a 1 Mb window, landing on the 2 kb rung, it takes 4.6 s, 7.6 MB and 17 requests where it took 8.0 s, 26.7 MB and 42.

  • The lab’s bucket holds only demo/yeast/, demo/store/ and demo/reassemblies/, and everything that read the rest reads the store (§24.3). The lab deleted the bucket’s other demo folders and the DNA Bacterial Zoo’s. The store’s files copied from them — the Gibcus et al. maps from demo/chicken/, the paper’s B. subtilis, P. aeruginosa and C. necator data from demo/subtilis/, demo/pak/ and the zoo — are now the only copies, and the store’s manifest names each as its own source, which scripts/publish-store.sh finds there and never copies (and fails on, saying so, if it is gone). The store gains ecoli/, E. coli K-12’s sequence and genes for the documentation’s screenshots. scripts/publish-demo.sh no longer uploads demo/ecoli/, demo/human/, demo/metagenomics/ or demo/yeast-mpneumoniae/, which stay in the repository for the tests and the gallery. The documentation’s screenshot scripts and the gallery’s read the store’s copies, and those whose files are gone everywhere — Nagano et al.’s .scool, the E. coli .pairs.gz, the chicken genes and .hic, progenitors.mcool and the zoo’s PAK map — ask for a copy by name rather than read a 404.

  • The landing page’s Human Hi-C data and Comparing data read from the lab’s bucket (§24.11). Every file either opens is now a copy in the lab’s store in Paris rather than on 4DN’s, IGV’s and 1000 Genomes’ buckets in Virginia and ENCODE’s in Oregon. Opening an example is a chain of round trips, and from Europe one is about 10 ms to Paris against 85 to 150 ms to those: for a reader in Paris (a modelled network), Human Hi-C data’s first picture in 0.9 s rather than 1.6. Comparing data’s files were in the store already; Human Hi-C data’s are listed under k562/ and the bucket needs scripts/publish-store.sh --only k562/.

  • A comparison opens both its maps at once (§5.7, §6.1, §12). A link or a workspace naming two maps opened the second once the first had landed and its view had rendered: two walks of a dozen dependent reads, one after the other. The second’s open now starts beside the first’s, with its own read plan; the first map is drawn alone as soon as it can be and the second triangle joins it; and a view’s two blocks are read together. The landing page’s Comparing data ships the read plans of both its maps, and draws its upper triangle in 1.2 s and both in 1.9 s, where they took 3.6 and 5.7 over the real network.

  • A gene table no longer holds up the lanes beside it (§8.3, §12). One worker opens a tab’s tracks and answers every lane’s request for its window, and it parsed a whole text file in one stretch: the ten bigWig lanes of the landing page’s Human Hi-C data waited for IGV’s RefSeq genes, 166,923 transcripts, before any of them was drawn. A gene table, a GTF, a BED and a BEDPE are now parsed in slices of 8 ms with the thread handed back between them, and so is the inflate before them, so a lane is answered between two slices. The parse is faster as well, 1.9 s to 0.8 s on those transcripts — each exon list read once rather than twice, a number a character at a time, a tabix index’s offsets without a BigInt each. For a reader in Paris, the bigWig lanes are drawn at 2.0 s rather than 4.0 s and the gene lane at 3.2 s rather than 3.7 s.

  • A remote map reads in quarter-megabyte chunks, not megabyte ones (§6.1, §12). Opening a cooler walks HDF5 metadata a few hundred bytes at a time, scattered across the file, and each place cost a whole chunk. The 7.9 GB 4DN map of K562 behind the landing page’s Human Hi-C data (§24.11) read 24 MB in 21 requests before its first picture at MYC; it now reads 9.7 MB in 25. Read-ahead is counted in bytes, so a stretch read chunk after chunk still runs a megabyte ahead and doubles to 16 MiB a fetch: a whole chromosome is about even (chromosome 2 of that map at 50 kb, 47 requests and 92 MB where it was 35 and 102, on screen in 24 s either way through a 100 ms, 50 Mbit/s link). The tracks already read in 256 KiB chunks.

  • The circularity check passes over a sequence longer than 10 Mb (§8.15). The circles people map are under it — a bacterial chromosome is about 4 Mb at the median, Myxococcus xanthus’s and Bradyrhizobium japonicum’s about 9.1 — and the few above are niche (the myxobacteria Sorangium and Minicystis, 13–16 Mb), left for the reader to tick. A eukaryote’s chromosome was three reads of a large file for an answer known in advance: 28 MB behind the first picture of the K562 map, beside every track being fetched, where nothing is read now. The app’s first-open check and chromosomix circular both leave such a sequence as it was. With the smaller chunk, the Human Hi-C data map’s reads end in about 5 s through that link, where they took 22 s, and it moves 9.7 MB where it moved 61.

  • A large block’s first picture is drawn at the size it is looked at (§7.2, §12). A block of 2,048² cells or more used to be transformed, measured, coloured into an image at matrix resolution, put on a canvas and halved down to the level the plot draws — all on the main thread, before the first frame. Its first frame now paints only the level it draws, straight from the values (render/levels.ts): each cell coloured exactly as before and averaged into the pixel it falls in, with the colour range the same number to the bit, read as order statistics of the raw values. A finer level is painted when a zoom first needs it, between frames, while the nearest level made stands in; an export paints the level it needs at once. Measured from a chromosome clicked in its table, against a server answering as S3 does: mouse chr1 at 20 kb on screen in 4.3 s where it took 14.2, yeast chrIV at 100 bp in 4.2 s where it took 26.6, chicken chr1 at the 25 kb a .hic opens at in 2.9 s where it took 6.3. A level painted from the values is the mean of its cells throughout, where a canvas halving an odd-sized level drifted into sampling one cell in two: such a map is smoother at fit zoom than it was, and no brighter or darker. The page’s report of a finished picture waits for a level still being painted when a report is due, and a zoom alone still makes none.

  • A sparse block is read once, and only its contacts after that (§6.4, §7.2). One pass over a large block lists the cells that carry something, each with its value, and sets apart the rows and columns a balancing left NaN; the colour range and every level are then read from that list (listCells), the same numbers and the same pixels to the bit. Balancing leaves a zero its weights cannot change where it is (weighBlock), bit for bit the product it was. Medians of three, from a chromosome clicked in its table against a server answering as S3 does: mouse chr1 at 20 kb on screen in 4.4 s where it took 4.9, its main-thread work 0.6 s where it was 1.3; yeast chrIV at 100 bp in 3.4 s where it took 4.1; chicken chr1 at 25 kb in 2.1 s where it took 2.5. A block in which more than one cell in four carries something is read whole, as before.

  • With Refine on zoom off and nothing picked, a chromosome is shown first from a coarser rung while its own block reads (§7.2, §12, §18.4). Where a stored rung puts at least 256 cells across the window and the block asked for is at least four times finer, that rung is read first and drawn — its own cells, resolution and colour range — and it is live: pan, zoom and hover work on it at once, with a spinner turning in its corner. The block lands on the window the reader moved it to, carried to the base pair, or refitted where nobody moved it, its colour range measured afresh either way. The zoom ladder, the Export card and every analysis wait for the block, since an analysis framed on the preview would count another rung’s bins. From the click on the row, against a server answering as S3 does: chicken chr1 at 25 kb shows its 500 kb preview in 0.2 s where the first picture took 2.0, its own block at 2.2 s; mouse chr1 at 80 kb shows 640 kb in 0.7 s where it took 2.3, its block at 2.9, the preview read first by the same reader. The preview is not the finer block made smaller: its cells are sums of many, so its compartments show more strongly than the block’s. A window already as coarse as a preview, a file with no coarser rung, a resolution picked or added and a map under a second file get none.

  • The census and the circularity check wait for the first map (§6.5, §8.6, §12). A .hic’s census started beside the open and the circularity check as the Chromosomes table showed, so the reader’s first click found a step of one or the other in flight and waited for it: chicken chr1 a moment after its table took 2.1–2.6 s where the same click later took 2.2. Both now wait for the first block of the first map — on a landing table, the map a row opens — or four seconds, as a file’s tracks already did; the 1D browser, whose picture is its tracks, still lets them go at once.

  • A remote map opened before opens all at once, and Human Hi-C data ships its own (§6.1, §12, §24.11). Opening a map over the network is a walk — each read names the place of the next — and the example’s map was 27 requests in a row before its first picture, at 0.15–0.35 s each over the real buckets. The reader now writes down the chunks an open and its first window touch, and the next open of the same map fetches them side by side as it starts, so the walk runs out of the cache; the example ships its plan, so a first visitor gets the same. Pressing Human Hi-C data: the map on screen in 1.5 s where it took 5.4, the Opening… line gone at 1.2 s where it stood for 3.3. A map reached through an .mcool’s root keeps its plan under both addresses: the lab’s progenitors.mcool lists its chromosomes in 0.85 s on a second visit where the first took 1.5.

  • The probe is the first read, and it goes out beside the HEAD (§6.1). It asked for one byte, after the HEAD had answered; it now asks for the file’s first chunk — what the first read of any container wants — at the same moment as the HEAD, and keeps it. A range is kept on the length the page can read where a bucket hides Content-Range, as 4DN’s does.

  • /bins is listed after the map’s first picture (§8.12). Listing a cooler’s bin columns for the Metadata card’s drop-down was four round trips the map waited for before its view was mounted.

Removed

Section titled “Removed”
  • The mock microbial community from the lab’s store (a dev feature, §29). Its files were deleted from the lab’s bucket, so the store’s Mock microbial community figure and its .metacool and .mmetacool rows went with them. The files stay in the repository, where the tests read them.

Fixed

Section titled “Fixed”
  • A wheel or pinch zoom aimed at the diagonal stays on it (§7.2, §8.9). A zoom keeps the point under the pointer fixed, and a pointer a fraction of a pixel off the diagonal carried the view’s centre off it by that fraction times the zoom, notch after notch: on the whole of K562 chr5, 263 kb a pixel, a pointer 0.8 px off the line ended a wheel zoom to 1 Mb about 200 kb off the diagonal. It looked as if the sharper blocks landing were shifting the picture, and they were not: measured, each landing keeps the window to the base pair. A zoom whose pointer is within four pixels of the diagonal is now about the diagonal’s nearest point, and the same zoom stays on it to the base pair from the whole chromosome to 1 Mb; a pointer further off is zoomed about exactly as before. The diagonal lock still holds every zoom to the line, near or not, and a turned map and a block the diagonal does not cross are left as they were.
  • The whole-genome view’s second reading below the diagonal is read on the grid of the block above it (§5.10, §7.2). It was read on a grid of its own, a block of another shape, its cells laid under the wrong half of the picture. Every view’s first read and every read its zoom makes are now planned by one function and sent whole, the rung and its pooling, rather than left for the reader to fit.
  • A comparison keeps its second file as you zoom (§5.7). The zoom asked both files for the first file’s other rungs, which the second need not store, and the second answered from its open map: the first refine drew the pair on two grids, and the comparison was gone from the picture. It reads the open rung only, pooled to the screen. A pick of another stored rung re-opens the file at it, carrying the second file to the same rung or putting it down, as a change of rung always did; a resolution summed from the open rung is drawn for both; and a pick on another rung that a link names is left aside while a second file is open.
  • A map opened at a resolution you added can be picked back to a finer one (§3.4). The view kept the added resolution as a floor of its own, so picking the rung it was summed from, read in place, went on drawing the sum.
  • Picking a resolution on the whole-genome view changes the picture (§5.5). The view was rebuilt for a pick rather than read in place, and its picture, kept by a key that named an added resolution but not a stored one, came back as it was. It is read in place now, as in the other views, and the key names the resolution. A view with nothing drawn yet — behind its question, or still reading — is rebuilt at the pick.
  • A pick survives a move to another chromosome, a bookmark, the deck and a re-open (§3.4, §8.10, §16.2). A chromosome opened from the table, the minimap or < and > went back to the zoom’s resolution; a bookmark re-opened the file at its rung’s entry; the deck’s companions followed the rung the first map was opened at, so a pick read in place on it left them where they were — they follow the rung picked now, the stored one under a resolution you added; and match all resolutions and the whole-genome view’s open at … instead re-opened the file at another entry under a pick the link still carried. Both pick now, in place.
  • The resolution row says what the map in front is drawn at (§5.5). Put another view in front — a whole-genome picture kept from before, a table — and the row went on naming the last map’s resolution until a block landed.
  • Taking off a resolution you added while it is picked picks the rung it was summed from (§3.4). It dropped to the rung the file was opened at, which may be another rung of the ladder altogether.
  • A task is lent the block on screen only when it was read from the rung the task reads (§18.4). A task queued before a refine could be lent the block of another rung, and framed on its bins.
  • A run’s label names the entry it read (§16.10, §18.4). It named the entry the file was open at, sample.mcool::10000 over a run of 25 kb bins; a map you named keeps its name.
  • Closing a tab, or opening another file in it, lets go of its resolution row (§5.5). The row listens for refine on zoom, and the last one drawn went on listening, and repainting, after the bar it was on had gone.
  • A pick finer than the open rung asks first on the whole-genome view (§9). Any stored rung read instead of the open one’s pixels passed the question by, a finer one too, which reads more of them rather than fewer.
  • A block read around the view as wide as a block may be no longer reads itself again on every landing, with refine on zoom off (§2). Clipped to the most a block may hold, it left the view nearer its edges than the read-ahead margin, so each landing asked for the same block again.
  • An analysis run on a map read from another rung reads the stretch on screen (§18.4). Its framing counted the rung’s bins under the open map’s name, so a task read the wrong window of the map and took the rung’s block for the file’s own bins; it is framed in the bins of the rung it runs at now, the block’s extents carried onto them through the base pairs they cover, one sequence at a time, and borrows only a block of that rung. A pooled view’s framing also printed its cell as the bin size — 80 kb bins · 4 per drawn cell for a 20 kb file — and names the rung’s own now.
  • A region opened on part of a sequence no longer reads nine times its window the moment it opens, with Refine on zoom on (§2). A view showing its whole block is pressed against every edge of it, and that was taken for a reader pushing against one.
  • A float-count map’s sum is its sum, not its floor (§3.2). The File card read a .metacool’s counted attributes through Python’s int(), as metacooler’s MetaCooler.info did, and so showed 60816 for a map whose counts sum to 60816.5. metacooler now keeps a fractional value (its c3d91ce), and so does the reader; the goldens metacooler builds are rebuilt with it.
