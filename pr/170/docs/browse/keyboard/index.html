Keyboard and command palette

Every action in chromosomix is a command with a name, and the ones you reach for often have a key. The command palette lists every command; the shortcuts dialog lists every key.

The command palette

Section titled “The command palette”

Press Ctrl+P (⌘P on a Mac), even from a box you are typing in, or click Search commands… in the header.

It lists every command, with what it does and its key, grouped as App, File, Go, View and Help, and Structure for a 3D model. A command that cannot act right now is greyed, not hidden. A switch is listed once, with on or off beside it, such as Crosshair on.

  • Type to narrow the list, best matches first; letters in order are enough, so wg finds Whole genome.
  • Start with a group’s name to search within it: file copy finds Copy this plot to the clipboard.
  • ↑ and ↓ choose, Enter or a click runs, and Esc closes.

Some commands have no key:

Group Commands with no key
File Add a file to this tab, Connect a local or remote folder (Connect a folder in the single-file version), Open a different file, Close this tab, Share this tab as a link, Add a map beside this one, and in the 1D browser Open a contact map on this genome
View Shrink the tracks to the shortest of them, Legend, Refine on zoom, Put every card back in its own panel
Help What this browser is remembering, Guided tutorial (not in the single-file version), About this viewer, Documentation, Source on GitHub
Structure Mark a bead, Mark a loop, Measure

The shortcuts dialog

Section titled “The shortcuts dialog”

Press ?, or the keyboard button at the right of the header, for Keyboard shortcuts: every key, in the palette’s groups, as your computer writes it, then what the mouse and keys do On a contact map and On the genome browser. It follows the tab in front, so a map and the 1D browser list a few keys differently.

How keys are read

Section titled “How keys are read”
  • Ctrl and ⌘. The palette, saving and copying use Ctrl on Windows and Linux and ⌘ on a Mac. Moving between tabs and opening the console use Control everywhere, a Mac included.
  • Letters work whatever their case, and with Caps Lock on.
  • − + < > are read by the character they type, from either half of their key: on an English keyboard , and . step between sequences without Shift, and = and _ change the resolution.
  • The digits and the key left of 1 are read by where they are. On a French keyboard the bare & é " ' keys are 1 to 4, and the keypad’s digits work too.
  • A bare key never acts with Ctrl, ⌘ or Alt held, AltGr included.
  • While you type in a box, keys type. Only the chords with Ctrl or ⌘ still act: the palette, saving, moving between tabs and the console.
  • Ctrl+C and Space give way. With text selected or the caret in a box, Ctrl+C copies the text. Space presses a focused button or tickbox rather than locking the diagonal.

A button’s tooltip also prints the key that does the same.

Folding the panels

Section titled “Folding the panels”

The digit row folds and opens the side columns, in the order they stand: the key left of 1 for Files, outermost on the left, then 1 General, 2 Annotations, 3 Aesthetics and 4 Analysis, outermost on the right. The key left of 1 is a backtick on an English keyboard, ² on a French PC and @ on a French Mac. The Files column exists only once you have connected a folder; the Annotations and Analysis columns start folded.

Drag a card by its heading into any open column; Escape during the drag puts it back. With the focus on a card’s heading, Alt+↑ and Alt+↓ (⌥↑ and ⌥↓) move it one place. Put every card back in its own panel, in the palette, undoes every move.

Tab walks the fields of one kind

Section titled “Tab walks the fields of one kind”

In a name or number edited where it is drawn, Tab keeps what you typed, as Enter does, and moves to the next field of the same kind; Shift+Tab moves to the one before. Past either end of the run, the caret is let go. It walks:

  • a track’s name, and the two ends of its range, beside a map and in the 1D browser;
  • the two ends of a map’s colour key, and of a 3D model’s key;
  • a tab’s name;
  • the heading of a cell of the deck, and a name on the Tracks, Metadata annotations, Structures or Contact maps card;
  • the place of a mark on the Manual annotations card.

Beside a map, a run goes through the tracks under it, then clockwise round it; in the 1D browser, down the tracks; on a deck, cell by cell as they stand. A range goes from its bottom to its top, then to the next track’s bottom.

A refused value keeps the caret. A bound you only tab through stays automatic. Only a bare Tab walks: Ctrl+Tab is still your browser’s.

Other keys

Section titled “Other keys”
  • A tab’s name: F2 or a double-click to rename, Enter to keep the name, Esc to abandon it.
  • Dialogs close with Esc, and so do the Analysis drawer, the stores drawer and the console.
  • Placing marks: Esc stops placing them, as done does, and Shift while drawing a rectangle locks it to the diagonal (Annotations and bookmarks).
  • A 3D model: drag to turn, scroll to zoom, Shift and drag to move it, ⌘ or Ctrl and a click on a bead to turn about it (a tap alone shows or hides the centre), Space and scroll for the fog, the arrow keys to turn it once its canvas has the focus, and Esc to let go of a half-made loop or measurement (3D models beside the map).
  • A task’s figure: the wheel, a drag, Shift and the wheel, and ⌘ or Ctrl with a drag zoom and pan it (Figures and export).
