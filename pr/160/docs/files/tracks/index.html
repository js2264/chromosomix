Tracks and features

A track is anything laid along the genome. Drop one on an open map and it is drawn beside the map, on any of its four edges; open one with no map and chromosomix opens the 1D browser over the genome the file describes (The genome browser). Each track is a row of the Tracks card in the Annotations panel, whose Load a track zone takes a drop or a click.

A file is told by what is in it, not only by its name, so a .txt of peaks or a headed .tsv of calls opens as what it is. A track’s sequences are matched to the map’s even when the two spell them differently (chr1 against 1, see Reference sequences), and the row says when they do not all match: 10 of 16 sequences matched; nothing is drawn on XI, XII, ….

Kind Formats Drawn as
Signal bigWig, bedGraph, wig a value track
Intervals BED, narrowPeak, broadPeak, gappedPeak, bigBed features
Gene models GTF, GFF3, genePred, genePredExt, refFlat, bigGenePred transcripts with their exons
Variants VCF the variants somebody called
Reads BAM, with its .bai or .csi depth, and the reads themselves
Methylation bedMethyl, bigMethyl percentage modified
Charts barChart, bigBarChart a small bar chart on each feature
Ideograms cytoBand, cytoBandIdeo each chromosome’s bands
Splice junctions STAR’s SJ.out.tab, a junctions BED, leafcutter’s .junc arcs with read counts
Lengths chrom.sizes the genome itself

Pairs of loci — BEDPE, interact, longrange — are on Interactions, and FASTA and .2bit on Reference sequences.

Signal: bigWig, bedGraph and wig

Section titled “Signal: bigWig, bedGraph and wig”

A bigWig is read by range, from your disk or by address: each view reads the window on screen at the file’s own zoom level for that width, which is what makes ten bigWigs over a whole genome cheap.

A bedGraph and a wig are the same kind of track written as text. They are read whole, and the summary for a window is worked out when the window is drawn. A bedGraph is recognised strictly — exactly four columns, the fourth a number on every row, or a track type= line saying so — so a BED with a name column is never mistaken for one; a wig is recognised by its fixedStep or variableStep lines, and its 1-based positions are read as such.

All three draw as a value track, with its own limits, colour and style, and can share an axis with other tracks; see The genome browser.

Intervals: BED and bigBed

Section titled “Intervals: BED and bigBed”

A BED of 3, 4, 5, 6, 9 or 12 columns, a narrowPeak, broadPeak or gappedPeak, and a table with a header row whose first three columns are a sequence, a start and an end all open as features. A BED12 draws its blocks as exons, its thick span thicker. A BED is drawn beside the map, never on it.

A bigBed is read whole, once, and drawn exactly as the BED it holds. Past a million intervals it is cut off, and says so. A bigBed whose own schema says it is something more — a bigGenePred, a bigBarChart, a bigMethyl or a bigInteract — is read as that.

Gene models: GTF, GFF3 and genePred

Section titled “Gene models: GTF, GFF3 and genePred”

GTF and GFF3 are read as transcripts assembled from their exon and CDS rows: the coding span drawn thick and the untranslated ends thin, the stop codon counted as coding, as in UCSC’s tables. A bacterial GFF3 from Prokka, Bakta or DFAST, whose rows name no parent, is read a feature per row, under its locus_tag and its gene symbol. Rows that describe a whole sequence (region, source) are left out.

genePred, genePredExt and refFlat — every UCSC table download (refGene, ncbiRefSeq, knownGene), and the refFlat RNA-seq pipelines take — open as the same gene models, with or without the leading bin column of a database dump. A bigGenePred is read as the BED12 it is and drawn as a gene model.

You can load several gene models. The star on a gene model’s row marks the one the locus box searches when you type a gene’s name.

Called variants: VCF

Section titled “Called variants: VCF”

A VCF opens as a lane of the variants somebody called. A substitution is drawn in the colour of the base it changes to — the colours the sequence lane and a BAM’s mismatches use — and an insertion, a deletion or a symbolic allele (<DEL>, <DUP>) in grey, as wide as it is: a symbolic allele’s size is read from its INFO/END or INFO/SVLEN. Click a variant for its card: REF>ALT, its type, QUAL, filter, and the AF, DP and SVTYPE of its INFO.

  • A VCF whose header has ##contig lines opens the 1D browser on its own chromosomes, at their exact lengths.
  • Sample columns are counted, not drawn: the lane’s summary says, for example, 1,284 variants · 2,504 samples not drawn.
  • A bgzipped VCF with its .tbi is read only for the sequences the map holds.
  • A VCF is read as text into memory, so the whole-genome calls of one human sample are too large to open; split by chromosome, they open. A BCF is not read: bcftools view writes the VCF.

Reads: BAM

Section titled “Reads: BAM”

A BAM opens as two rows, both read from the window on screen:

  • The depth row, under the file’s name, is a value track like a bigWig, beside a map or in the browser. A bin holds the mean depth: a deletion counts as covered and a skipped intron does not, secondary and supplementary alignments are left out, and duplicates are kept unless you switch on hide duplicates.
  • The reads row, name · reads, is the pile of individual reads, drawn in the 1D browser only, where a read has bases to be drawn along.

min MAPQ and hide duplicates apply to both rows; the colouring, the layout of pairs and split reads to the pile alone. Where the reads disagree with the reference they are coloured, from the reads’ own MD or =/X codes, or from a FASTA open in the same tab. Base modifications come from the MM and ML tags a nanopore or PacBio basecaller writes: colour by → base modification on the pile, and % modified on the depth row. A spliced library draws the introns its reads skip as arcs over the depth (junction arcs, from two reads by default).

The index. A .bai or .csi beside the BAM is read once, and every window’s reads are then read directly. Without one, the reads are found by searching the sorted file, a handful of small reads a window; the row’s summary says reads located by search, and windows are answered one at a time, up to 4 Mb. A remote BAM’s index is looked for at <address>.bai; where it lives elsewhere, add #index=<address of the .bai>.

Zoomed out. A BAM has no summary to draw from, so a window too deep to read draws nothing and says why — 12.4 MB of reads — zoom in — and so does a pile asked for over too wide a window.

Refused. A BAM must be sorted by coordinate. One sorted by read name, or a Hi-C pipeline’s alignment file, which is in read-pair order, is refused with the commands that sort and index it:

For Hi-C, the .pairs or the contact map is the file to open. CRAM is not read: convert it with samtools view -b.

Methylation: bedMethyl and bigMethyl

Section titled “Methylation: bedMethyl and bigMethyl”

A bedMethyl — what modkit pileup writes, or the older ENCODE WGBS spelling — and a bigMethyl open as a track of percentage modified, one value per base, on an axis of 0 to 100 to begin with. Where a file holds several modifications over the same bases, 5mC and 5hmC say, the commonest is drawn and the lane’s summary lists every code in the file with its count of rows. Codes are named as the SAMtags specification names them: m 5mC, h 5hmC, a 6mA, and so on. A row with no reads under it is left out rather than drawn as 0 %.

Charts: barChart and bigBarChart

Section titled “Charts: barChart and bigBarChart”

UCSC’s barChart gives each feature a vector of values — expression across tissues or conditions — and each is drawn as a small bar chart where there is room, and as one box in the colour and height of its top category where there is not. The categories’ names and colours come from the file’s track line (barChartBars, barChartColors). A bigBarChart opened on its own has no such line, so its categories are numbered.

Every chart on a lane shares one ceiling, the file’s maxLimit or its largest value. scale each chart draws each against its own tallest bar instead, and typed limits put the lane on a fixed axis, which is how two files are compared. Click a feature for its whole chart, a row per category.

Ideograms: cytoBand

Section titled “Ideograms: cytoBand”

A cytoBand or cytoBandIdeo draws each chromosome as its Giemsa bands, pinched at the centromere and narrowed along a stalk, in the stains UCSC and IGV use. The line under the lane names the bands in the window (Xq27.3 – q28), a click names a band as a cytogeneticist would (Xq28), and the locus box finds bands by name beside genes.

A cytoBand whose bands tile every chromosome declares the genome: UCSC’s hg19_cytoBand.txt alone opens the browser over hg19’s sequences at their exact lengths.

Splice junctions

Section titled “Splice junctions”

Junctions are drawn as a sashimi plot’s arcs, from the end of one exon to the start of the next, each labelled with its read count. Three files are read:

File Written by Recognised by
SJ.out.tab STAR its nine integer columns
a junctions BED12 regtools, TopHat, Portcullis junc or sj in its name, a TopHat track line, or JUNC… row names
.junc leafcutter its name

What else a file says — STAR’s multi-mapping reads, motif, annotated and overhang, or a BED’s anchors — is on each junction’s card and can colour the lane, so colouring by annotated maps novel splicing. A file with junctions on both strands splits the lane, forward arcs above its middle and reverse arcs below. The lane’s lower limit is a filter, like a sashimi plot’s minimum junction coverage. A spliced BAM draws its own arcs, from the reads (above).

Lengths: chrom.sizes

Section titled “Lengths: chrom.sizes”

A chrom.sizes — two columns, a name and a length, as UCSC writes it, or a .genome file of the same shape — declares a genome. Opened on its own, it starts the 1D browser on those sequences. Dropped on a browser that was started from a feature file, whose lengths could only be guessed from its furthest feature (the Genome card then says inferred (rounded up)), it gives the real ones.

Reading part of a large text file

Section titled “Reading part of a large text file”

Text formats — BED, GTF, GFF3, bedGraph, VCF and the rest — are read whole. Bgzipped and indexed with tabix, a text file is read only for the sequences the open map holds: a human GTF beside a map of one chromosome reads about a twentieth of the file.

Keep the .tbi beside the file, drop the two together, or name it with #index=<address>. The index is only ever a saving: a plain .gz, a bgzipped file without one, and an index that is missing, damaged or for another assembly all fall back to reading the whole file — and so does an index that would save less than a fifth of it.

A browser cannot hold much more than half a gigabyte of text, so a larger file cannot be read whole: index it, or split it by chromosome.

The file’s own columns

Section titled “The file’s own columns”

Colouring by a column. A feature lane is coloured by strand to begin with. Its settings’ colour by offers the file’s other columns too — a GTF’s biotype and source, a narrowPeak’s signal, a VCF’s QUAL, filter and SVTYPE, a BED’s name column — as categories in a fixed palette, commonest first, or, for numbers, a colour ramp over the file’s whole range, which does not change as you pan. Names that are exactly A and B take the compartments’ red and blue.

The map’s own columns. A cooler keeps columns beside its pixels: at least the balancing weight in its bins table, and whatever else the tool that wrote it added. The Metadata annotations card, folded at the foot of the Annotations panel, offers them: pick a column and press add, and it is drawn as a lane like any track — numbers as bars, labels as runs of colour. A column is offered where the view on screen can show it, and its row exports it as a TSV, a row per bin or sequence as the file wrote it.
