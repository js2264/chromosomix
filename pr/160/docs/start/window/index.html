A tour of the window

The window has the same parts whatever you open. From the top: the header, the tabs, the trail and the heading over the picture, the picture itself — a contact map, a genome browser or a deck of cells — with the panels either side of it, the console along the bottom when it is open, and the status bar at the foot.

The header

Section titled “The header”

From left to right:

  • The brand, the mark and chromosomix. It takes you to the landing page — in a new tab when the one in front holds something, so nothing you have open is lost.
  • The stores, which opens the store drawer (\), and the Files column, which is there only while you have connected a folder (below).
  • The four panels’ buttons: General, Annotations, Aesthetics and Analysis, each folding its panel away or bringing it back. A panel with nothing to show for what is open is absent, and so is its button.
  • +, which adds a file to this tab: a second map comes up beside the one open, anything else is drawn on it as a track. On an empty tab it opens the file instead. It is the landing page’s Load files box in a dialog, for a file to drop, choose or name by its URL.
  • Back and forward, the browser’s own, as buttons. The chevron beside back lists the steps behind you, each named as the trail names it, and goes straight to one.
  • Share this tab as a link, Save every tab as a file (Ctrl+S, ⌘S on a Mac) and the console (Ctrl+`): the three ways to take the work out of the window. Sessions, workspaces and links says what the link and the file each keep.
  • Search commands…, the command search (below).
  • About the window: the theme (T), the keyboard shortcuts (?), this documentation (the open book), the source on GitHub, and About this viewer — what the app is, the guided tutorial, which build you are running, the notebooks that check it, and the licences of the projects it is built with.

A button that has a key names it in its tooltip. In a narrow window the header wraps onto two lines.

The command search

Section titled “The command search”

Search commands…, or Ctrl+P (⌘P on a Mac), opens the command palette: every action in the app, in five groups — App, File, Go, View and Help — each with its key where it has one. Type a few letters of a command’s name and press Enter. A command that cannot act on what is open is listed greyed rather than hidden, and a setting that is on or off wears its state beside its name.

Some things are only in the palette: Refine on zoom, which reads a window again at a finer grid when you zoom past what was read; Put every card back in its own panel; and, under Help, What this browser is remembering, the list of everything the app keeps in this browser, with a button that forgets it all. The shortcuts dialog (?) prints the same commands, those with a key, and the gestures on a map and in the genome browser. Keyboard and command palette has them all.

Tabs

Section titled “Tabs”

Under the header, a tab for each file you have open — twelve at most. Each tab is a session of its own: its file, its tracks, its marks, its settings. Two tabs on the same file share nothing.

  • The + at the end of the row, or O, opens a new tab on the landing page. Ctrl+< and Ctrl+> step to the tab beside this one.
  • A tab is named after its file. Double-click it, or press F2, to give it a name of your own; empty the box to give it back its file’s name.
  • Its × closes it, as a middle-click does. The last tab is emptied rather than closed.

The address bar names what the tab in front is showing; for a file on the web, that address is already a link to the view. Close the browser and the window offers your tabs back next time, with restore; it never reopens them unasked. A bar under the tabs carries what a tab found out about its file — that a sequence reads as circular, say — until you dismiss it or the tab opens another file.

Over the picture: the trail and the heading

Section titled “Over the picture: the trail and the heading”

The trail says where you are and takes you back up: the file — click it to open another file in its place — then the table or the whole genome, the sequence, and the window. At its far end are two controls over the whole picture:

  • resolution, the sizes of bin the file stores: a slider over labelled stops, or a dropdown on a file with more than nine. The + beside it adds a resolution the file does not store, made by adding up its own bins and marked with a *. - and + step coarser and finer.
  • contacts, which draws every map in the tab to the number of contacts you type, 10M say, to compare libraries at one depth.

The heading names what is on screen: the title, with ‹ and › for the previous and next sequence (< and >); the locus box and go; two arrows that move the window half its width; the zoom steps, 10× and 3× out, 3× and 10× in; and a bookmark of the view (B). The line under it counts the bins on screen and says what one of them is worth, its effective resolution: coarser than the file’s when the window holds more bins than the picture has room for. Moving around covers all of it.

The picture, and the deck of cells

Section titled “The picture, and the deck of cells”

A tab shows one of three pictures:

  • A contact map, with tracks along any of its four edges and marks on it. See Reading a contact map.
  • The genome browser: tracks, sequences and features along the genome, with no map. A tab opens as one when its first file is a track rather than a map. See The genome browser.
  • A deck: several cells side by side, panned and zoomed together.

A deck is opened from the map itself. A + stands down its right-hand edge and another along its bottom; each opens a column or a row of empty cells, and an empty cell takes a map, a track or a 3D structure dropped on it. A contact map dropped on an open map opens beside it in the same way.

Each cell’s heading carries a grip, to drag the cell onto another and swap the two; its name; a star, which says whose settings the Map card shows and which map an analysis runs on; and a chain, lit while the cell follows the others’ navigation. The deck’s own bar, above the grid, holds add a cell… (a map from a file or a URL, the tab’s 3D structure, or tracks alone), match all scales, match all resolutions, how many cells across, the whole deck as one figure, and, at its far end, close deck. Maps side by side has the rest.

The pictures keep their light colours in the dark theme, as they will have in a figure.

The four panels

Section titled “The four panels”

Two panels stand either side of the picture. Each is opened and folded by its button in the header, by its key, or by the chevron in its own heading; a folded panel leaves a narrow strip with its name on it, which opens it again.

Panel Key Where What it holds
General 1 left the file itself
Annotations 2 left, against the picture what is drawn on and beside it
Aesthetics 3 right, against the picture how it is drawn
Analysis 4 right what can be computed from it

General and Aesthetics are open when you first arrive; Annotations and Analysis start folded, so a laptop screen shows a map rather than four columns. The keys are matched by their place on the keyboard, so they work on any layout.

General

Section titled “General”

What the file is. The File card has its format, bin size, contacts and the rest; the Sequences card lists its chromosomes, each with a tick — one ticked is that chromosome’s map, several are those chromosomes against each other, none is the whole genome; the Genome minimap draws them with the view on screen marked; and Metadata holds whatever else the file stores.

Annotations

Section titled “Annotations”

Everything drawn on the picture or beside it, as rows you can work on. Contact maps lists the maps of the tab. Tracks holds every track — loaded or computed — with Load a track at its top; each row sets its colour, height, range and style, and four small squares place it on any edge of the map. Structures lists the 3D models. Manual annotations holds the marks you draw — lines, arrowheads and the like — and Metadata annotations the file’s own columns; Bookmarks keeps places to come back to. See Annotations and bookmarks.

Aesthetics

Section titled “Aesthetics”

How the picture is drawn. The Map card sets the score (counts, balanced and the normalisations), the smoothing, O/E, the transform, the colours, the clip and the overlays; in a deck it is the starred map’s card, and says so under its title. The Tracks card sets how the tracks are drawn, and Export saves the picture as a figure, a PDF or a PNG. See Figures and export.

Analysis

Section titled “Analysis”

A list of tasks, in three groups: 2D tasks read the map, 3D tasks model it, 1D tasks read the tracks. Each row has a line saying what the task answers and its state: never run, queued, running, done, failed or cancelled. A box over the list filters it by name or by what the task is for. A row waiting on a file is greyed and says what to load. Hover the ? at the end of a row’s first line for the published method the task reproduces and how it compared with it; the ? is green where it matched.

Pressing a row opens the Analysis drawer over the right of the window: the task’s settings, run (and cancel while it runs), its progress, and its results, with their exports. The drawer is not a dialog: the map stays live behind it, every key keeps working, and Esc or its × puts it away without stopping the run. See Analyses.

Folding, resizing and moving

Section titled “Folding, resizing and moving”

Drag a panel’s inner edge to widen it; double-click that edge to give it back its width. Each card folds by its heading, and can be dragged by its heading to another place, in its own panel or another — the Export card next to the Tracks card, say. The palette’s Put every card back in its own panel undoes the moves. The panels’ folds, widths and arrangement are remembered by this browser.

The Files column

Section titled “The Files column”

A fifth column, outermost on the left, lists the places you have connected — a folder on your disk, a bucket, a Google Drive folder, a Dropbox — one level at a time. It appears once you connect one, from the landing page or with Connect a local or remote folder in the command palette, and its own connect a local or remote folder button connects more. It is the same in every tab. The key left of 1 folds it.

Each file row has two buttons, + to add the file to the tab in front and a second to open it in a tab of its own, and a row can be dragged onto the map, onto one of its edges or onto a cell. A folder on your disk connected in a browser tab lasts the visit, and its row says this visit; the desktop app, and the web app installed from Chrome or Edge, keep it across restarts (Web, desktop or one file). See Opening files.

The store drawer

Section titled “The store drawer”

The stores’ button in the header, or \, opens the store drawer down the left of the window, over the panels: one catalogue of published data at a time — the lab’s figures, IGV’s genomes, 4DN, ENCODE, DNA Zoo, Roadmap, UCSC track hubs and any store you added — with a row of small tiles at its top to switch between them. It stays up while you work beside it, and Esc or its × puts it away. See Data stores.

The console

Section titled “The console”

The console opens along the bottom of the window, from its button in the header or with Ctrl and the key left of 1 (Ctrl+` on most keyboards, Control on a Mac too). Every file opened, track loaded, analysis run and file written is a line in it, as the chromosomix command that repeats it, grouped by day. copy, save and clear act on the whole log, and provenance prints each run’s full record above it.

The log is kept for a week, or five hundred lines, in this browser, and never sent anywhere. Like the drawer, the console is not a dialog: the map behind it stays live, and Esc puts it away. See The console.

The status bar

Section titled “The status bar”

At the foot of the window, from the left:

  • the coordinates under the pointer, on the map or on a track;
  • read, how much of the file has been read, with the requests behind it on hover;
  • validation, a deeper check of the file’s stored pixels, which says never run until you press it;
  • memory and cpu, what this tab is costing your machine.

read and validation are there only while a file is open. Hover any of them for its detail.
