Glossary

The words chromosomix uses on screen and these pages use about it, in alphabetical order. Where a page explains a word at length, its entry links there.

Aggregate peak analysis (APA)

Section titled “Aggregate peak analysis (APA)”

The contact map piled up around a set of loops or features — every one of them cut out and averaged — against a control, to show whether they are enriched as a group. See Aggregate peak analysis.

Allow access

Section titled “Allow access”

The button a tab shows when Chrome or Edge remembers a local file it was reading and needs only your permission to read it again, after a restart for instance. One press allows every file the browser remembers. See Sessions, workspaces and links.

Arms, origin and terminus

Section titled “Arms, origin and terminus”

A bacterial chromosome’s replication origin, set per file in the Chromosomes table or by the Origin and terminus task, divides it into two arms that meet again at the terminus. Where the arms are held side by side, the map has a second diagonal through the origin. See Origin and terminus and Inter-arm alignment.

Assembly

Section titled “Assembly”

A genome as a list of sequences and their lengths, with or without a contact map behind it — read from a FASTA, a .2bit, a chrom.sizes, the header of a bigWig, a bigBed or a BAM, a cytoBand, or a map. It is what the genome browser is built on. See Reference sequences.

Balancing

Section titled “Balancing”

Correcting a contact map for how visible each bin is — how many contacts it makes in all — so that its rows and columns can be compared. A file can carry its own weights, which the balanced score reads; chromosomix can also compute VC, square-root VC and ICE itself. balance=true in a link. See Reading a contact map.

Batch

Section titled “Batch”

The box that opens above the list of the Tracks card when you tick two rows or more: a setting changed there applies to every ticked row, and reads mixed where they differ. See Annotations and bookmarks.

Bin

Section titled “Bin”

A fixed stretch of a sequence, the unit a contact map’s rows and columns are made of: at 5 kb, a 100 kb window is 20 bins across. A map’s resolution is its bin size.

Bookmark

Section titled “Bookmark”

A saved place, kept with the resolution it was found at, on the Bookmarks card. See Annotations and bookmarks.

Bucket

Section titled “Bucket”

An object store — Amazon S3, Cloudflare R2, Google Cloud Storage and their kin — that serves files by byte range. A file on one opens by its address, and a prefix of one can be connected as a folder with connect a bucket…, with a key if it is private. See Hosting your data.

Called set

Section titled “Called set”

The places a task found — boundaries, domains, peaks, A/B segments, sites, loops — as one row of the Tracks card. It is drawn either as a 1D track beside the map or as an overlay on the map it was called from, and a set of loops is a ring round each pair. See How analyses run.

Card

Section titled “Card”

One of the titled boxes in a side panel: Tracks, Contact maps, Bookmarks, Map and the rest. Drag a card by its heading to move it to another panel; Put every card back in its own panel, in the command palette, undoes that. See A tour of the window.

Cell

Section titled “Cell”

One place in a deck: a map, an empty cell waiting for a file, a structure cell drawing a 3D model, or a tracks cell drawing the tab’s tracks on their own. An empty cell takes a map, a track or a structure dropped on it. See Maps side by side.

Circular sequence

Section titled “Circular sequence”

A sequence you declare to join its own ends — a bacterial chromosome, a plasmid — by its tick in the circular column of the Chromosomes table. A file’s first opening ticks the sequences whose contacts across the join look like a circle’s. A window may then cross the join, and most tasks measure the distance between two places the short way round.

Command palette

Section titled “Command palette”

Ctrl+P (⌘+P on a Mac): every command of the app by name, with its key where it has one. ? lists the keys. See Keyboard and command palette.

Comparison

Section titled “Comparison”

A second map drawn in the lower-left triangle of the same square, on one colour scale, so that the difference is read across the diagonal — or the two maps’ ratio or difference over the whole square. The two must share a bin size and a list of sequences; a map that shares only coordinates goes beside it in a deck instead. See Comparing two maps.

Compartments

Section titled “Compartments”

The checkerboard of a Hi-C map: the genome sorted into two kinds of chromatin, A and B, each in contact with its own kind. The Compartments task finds them as the leading components of the observed over expected — E1, whose sign, phased by genes, a track or GC content, says which is which — with a saddle plot. See Compartments.

Connected folder

Section titled “Connected folder”

A place the window keeps and lists one level at a time in the Files panel: a folder on your computer, a bucket prefix, a Google Drive folder or your Dropbox. A file in it opens with a click, or drags onto a map, a cell or a card. In a browser tab a folder on your computer is connected for the visit (this visit); an installed app keeps it. See Opening files.

Console

Section titled “Console”

What the window has done, written as the commands that would do it again: each analysis you run becomes the chromosomix command that repeats it, beside the files opened and written. Opened with Ctrl+`, the key left of 1, and kept in this browser for a week, 500 lines at most. See The console.

Contacts box

Section titled “Contacts box”

The contacts box in the bar over the map draws every map of the tab as though it held that many contacts: a map with more is subsampled exactly, one with fewer oversampled with Boost-HiC, so that maps of different depths can be compared. Empty, every map is read as it is stored. contacts= in a link. See Comparing two maps.

Coordinates

Section titled “Coordinates”

A place in a genome is written two ways. What you read and type is 1-based and inclusive, digits grouped — IV:1,000,001-1,400,000, as IGV and UCSC print it — and the command line takes the same. A map’s region in a link is cooler’s form, 0-based and half-open with no separators — IV:1000000-1400000 — naming the same bases. See Link keys.

Deck

Section titled “Deck”

Cells side by side in one tab, panned and zoomed together. The + down the map’s right edge adds a column of cells and the one along its bottom a row. Its maps may differ in resolution, and need share only their coordinates. maps= in a link. See Maps side by side.

Desktop app

Section titled “Desktop app”

chromosomix as an application for macOS and Linux, from the releases page: the same viewer, on your disk, which can also read by range a host that compresses its files. See Web, desktop or one file.

Detached

Section titled “Detached”

A track from a local file after a reload: its settings came back and its bytes could not, since no browser reads a dropped file again without being handed it. Its row keeps its colours and offers to read the file again. A computed track after a reload offers recompute instead. See Sessions, workspaces and links.

Domain

Section titled “Domain”

A square along the diagonal: a stretch of sequence in more contact with itself than with its neighbours, often called a TAD. Insulation and the directionality index find the borders between domains; TAD annotation, Juicer’s Arrowhead, finds the domains, nested where they nest. See TAD annotation.

Edge

Section titled “Edge”

Which of a map’s four sides a track’s lane is on: top, right, bottom or left. A file dropped on the middle of a map instead is that map’s alone — drawn on its face if it is an overlay, on its usual edge if it is a track. See Annotations and bookmarks.

Embed

Section titled “Embed”

The viewer inside another web page, as an <iframe> whose address carries ?embed=1: one view of one file, with no header and no tabs, and the Annotations and Aesthetics panels folded at its sides. See Embedding in a web page.

Eye, × and grip

Section titled “Eye, × and grip”

The three controls on each row of the Contact maps, Tracks and Structures cards. The eye shows or hides, keeping every setting; the × lets go; the grip drags the row up or down its card, onto a cell, or onto a + edge to place it in a new cell there. See Maps side by side.

Facet

Section titled “Facet”

On a store’s page, one of its columns, down the side, with every value counted. Ticking values narrows the rows: values ticked in one facet add up, ticks in two facets narrow each other, and the count beside a value is what ticking it would add. See Data stores.

Feature set

Section titled “Feature set”

A track of intervals — peaks, genes, domains, any BED — that a task can take as its features: the anchors of an APA, the starts of a metaplot. The star on a feature set’s row of the Tracks card picks the set the feature search reads, and the one a task takes unless you choose another. See Tracks and features.

Framing and provenance

Section titled “Framing and provenance”

The framing is what a task runs on, taken when you press run: the window, the bin size, the score, the balancing, the contacts total, the circles and origins. It is a snapshot — pan away and the result stands, with an offer to compute it again. The provenance adds the task and its version, every parameter, the file, the build and the time; it is shown with the result and written into every export that can hold a comment. See How analyses run.

Genome browser

Section titled “Genome browser”

The 1D view: tracks along a sequence, with or without a contact map, at a locus you type or drag to. A track or a reference opened on its own starts one over its assembly; view=browser in a link. See The genome browser.

Group

Section titled “Group”

Tracks that belong together — a lane each, or overlaid in one — sharing one value axis, so that two strands of one experiment are read against one ceiling; set to each, every member keeps its own axis. On the Groups card. See Annotations and bookmarks.

Ideogram

Section titled “Ideogram”

A chromosome drawn from a UCSC cytoBand file: its outline, pinched at the centromere and filled with its stained bands. A band’s name, such as Xq28, can be typed in the locus box. See Tracks and features.

Insulation

Section titled “Insulation”

How much contact crosses each bin along the diagonal, measured in a diamond-shaped window slid along it. Its dips are the boundaries between domains, and the wider the window, the higher in the hierarchy of domains the boundaries it finds. The task Diamond insulation. See Diamond insulation.

Lane

Section titled “Lane”

One placement of a track: on one map, on one edge. A track can have several lanes — the same bigWig under two maps — or none, loaded but not drawn. A lane is the same height on every map, so the maps line up. See Annotations and bookmarks.

Lead and companion

Section titled “Lead and companion”

In a deck, the lead is the tab’s own file — the first map, which names the tab and holds its tables, its tracks and its comparison — and the companions are the maps beside it. The app calls the lead the first map. See Maps side by side.

Level

Section titled “Level”

What one row and column of the map stand for: a bin, or a whole sequence summed into one cell, which shows how much each chromosome contacts every other. level=contig in a link. See Reading a contact map.

Lock and chain

Section titled “Lock and chain”

What moves a deck’s maps together. The lock follows places in base pairs, so maps at different resolutions stay on the same place; the chain in each map’s heading says whether that map follows, and unchained, it moves on its own. See Maps side by side.

Locus

Section titled “Locus”

A window in the genome browser, written as genome browsers write one: chrI:1,001-20,000, 1-based and inclusive. The locus box also finds a gene or a band by its name. See Moving around.

Loop

Section titled “Loop”

A dot off the diagonal: two places in more contact than their distance predicts, often at the corner of a domain. Loop calling finds them as pixels enriched over their local background, and Pattern detection (Chromosight) by matching a template. See Loop calling.

Mark

Section titled “Mark”

An annotation you draw yourself — a line, an arrowhead or a rectangle on the map — typed as coordinates or picked on the picture. Marks are on the Manual annotations card, and leave as BED and BEDPE files. See Annotations and bookmarks.

Metadata annotation

Section titled “Metadata annotation”

A column the open file already carries about its own sequences or bins — a balancing weight, for one — drawn against the map like a track. On the Metadata annotations card. See Annotations and bookmarks.

O/E

Section titled “O/E”

Observed over expected: each cell divided by what its distance from the diagonal predicts, which takes away the fall of contact with distance and leaves what is unusual. A switch beside the score rather than a score of its own, usually read on balanced counts with a diverging colour map. oe=true in a link. See Reading a contact map.

P(s)

Section titled “P(s)”

Contact probability as a function of genomic separation s: how contact frequency falls with distance, the curve every Hi-C map shares and the expected that O/E divides by. The task Contact probability P(s). See Contact probability P(s).

Panel

Section titled “Panel”

One of the columns either side of the map: General (the file itself), Annotations (tracks, marks and bookmarks), Aesthetics (scales, colours and layout) and Analysis (the tasks), with Files beside them while a folder is connected. Each folds to a narrow strip and opens again. See A tour of the window.

Pinned release

Section titled “Pinned release”

A release of the app kept for good at its own address, https://chromosomix.app/v/<tag>/, which takes the same links and embeds as the root and goes on drawing the same picture. About lists the builds that are published. See Running your own copy.

Refine on zoom

Section titled “Refine on zoom”

A preference, in the command palette: zooming in past what the block in hand can show reads the window again at a finer resolution, one bounded read at a time. It is off by default, and zooming then magnifies what was read. See Moving around.

Resolution

Section titled “Resolution”

A map’s bin size: 5 kb, 1 Mb. An .mcool or a .hic stores several, and ::/resolutions/<bin size> names one in an address. A resolution the file does not store, made by summing whole bins of one it does, is marked *: 3 kb*. See Contact maps.

Score

Section titled “Score”

What the numbers in a map’s cells are: the raw counts, the balanced counts, VC, square-root VC or ICE computed on the fly, the expected count at that distance, the correlation the compartments are read from, or another column the file carries. score= in a link. See Reading a contact map.

Sequence

Section titled “Sequence”

One chromosome, contig or plasmid. The app says sequence in its own words, and chromosome or contig where it reports what a file calls one — the Chromosomes table of a map, for one. See Moving around.

Session

Section titled “Session”

What a tab holds — one file, its views, its tracks, its marks and its settings — called a tab on screen. These pages also call a tab written down a session: a pre-configured session in a store opens a map with its tracks already placed, and the landing page’s example sessions, under Pick an example session, open a picture each. See Sessions, workspaces and links.

Share link

Section titled “Share link”

A link to one tab, made by Share this tab as a link: the tab whole — its view, tracks, marks and bookmarks — written after the #, which no server sees. shorten with is.gd makes a short one through that service. See Sessions, workspaces and links.

Single file

Section titled “Single file”

The whole viewer as one HTML file, chromosomix-<tag>.html on every release, which opens from a disk with no server and no network, and reads local files only. See Web, desktop or one file.

Splice junction

Section titled “Splice junction”

An intron that reads were seen to skip, with how many said so — from STAR’s SJ.out.tab, a junctions BED, leafcutter’s .junc or a spliced BAM — drawn as a sashimi arc from one exon’s end to the next one’s start, as tall as its count. See Tracks and features.

Star

Section titled “Star”

Which map of a deck the Analysis panel reads, the Map card sets and the File card describes. Press the filled star to star none: the Map card then sets every map at once, and the tasks that read a map wait. A structure cell has a star of its own, and so does a feature set’s row. See Maps side by side.

Status bar

Section titled “Status bar”

The strip along the bottom of the window. Its read gives the requests and bytes this tab’s files have cost, in total and for the last view. See A tour of the window.

Store

Section titled “Store”

A catalogue of files somebody published, with columns to narrow by, drawn as a page in the store drawer: one JSON file, or a UCSC track hub read as one. Built in are RSG lab data, IGV genomes, 4DN open data, ENCODE, DNA Zoo and Roadmap; Add a custom store opens any other. See Data stores and Your own data store.

Store drawer

Section titled “Store drawer”

The panel down the left of the window where a store is browsed, opened by a store’s tile on the landing page, by the header’s toggle or by \. There is one for the whole window, so it stays as you left it when you switch tabs. See Data stores.

Structure and model

Section titled “Structure and model”

A structure is a 3D file of beads placed in space — g3d, .3dg, bed3d or PDB — listed on the Structures card and drawn in a structure cell beside the map. A model is one set of its coordinates, a chain the beads of one sequence, and a bead one row of the file. Model from the map makes one from the contacts. See 3D models beside the map.

Synthetic track

Section titled “Synthetic track”

A task’s result that became an ordinary track: a row of the Tracks card marked * after its name, with its exports in its settings. So is the ratio or difference of two tracks. After a reload its values are gone, and its row offers recompute. See How analyses run.

Tab

Section titled “Tab”

One session, in the strip under the header: one file and everything you did with it. o opens a new one, Ctrl+< and Ctrl+> step between them, and F2 or a double-click renames one; up to twelve can be open. See A tour of the window.

Task

Section titled “Task”

One analysis in the Analysis panel, listed with its state on the 2D tasks, 3D tasks or 1D tasks card. Pressed, it opens in the Analysis drawer — its parameters, run, its progress and its results — while the map stays live. A greyed task says what to load first. See How analyses run.

Track

Section titled “Track”

A 1D dataset you loaded — a bigWig, a BED, gene models, reads — with its name, colour and style, listed on the Tracks card and drawn in one or more lanes. See Tracks and features.

Track hub

Section titled “Track hub”

UCSC’s format for a published shelf of tracks: a hub.txt naming a genomes.txt, which names a trackDb.txt for each assembly. chromosomix reads one as a store, through Connect to a UCSC hub. See UCSC track hubs.

Transform and colour scale

Section titled “Transform and colour scale”

The transform is the scale a value is coloured on — log1p, log10, log2 or none — and the colour scale’s bottom and top (vmin, vmax) clip it, set automatically unless you set them. The colour map is the ramp itself; RdBu_r, BuWtRd and seismic are diverging, for a ratio or an O/E. See Reading a contact map.

Turn (the 45° view)

Section titled “Turn (the 45° view)”

The map of one sequence rotated 45°: position along the bottom, distance from the diagonal up the side. The turn is the deck’s, so it turns every map at once; flip=true in a link. See The 45° view.

Tutorial

Section titled “Tutorial”

The guided tutorial plays a first session on the live window, one step at a time, on 4DN’s Hi-C of K562 cells, greying everything but the control each step is about. It is offered on the landing page under New here?, in About, and in the command palette as Guided tutorial. See The guided tutorial.

View

Section titled “View”

What a tab shows of its file: the Chromosomes table, the whole genome, a region, a selection of sequences, the genome browser, or the cells of an .scool. view= in a link. See Link keys.

Virtual 4C

Section titled “Virtual 4C”

What one place touches: the contacts of an anchor with everything around it, as a track you can export. The task 4C profile from an anchor. See 4C profile from an anchor.

Window

Section titled “Window”

The browser tab, or the desktop app’s window, that chromosomix runs in, with its header, its strip of tabs, its panels, its console and its status bar. The theme, and each panel’s width and fold, belong to the window: the same whatever tab is in front. See A tour of the window.

Workspace

Section titled “Workspace”

Every open tab in one file, a .chromosomix.json, written by Save every tab as a file (Ctrl+S, ⌘+S on a Mac). Dropped on the window, its tabs open beside yours; published, it opens from ?config=<address> or from a store’s row. The browser also keeps your last one and offers it back with restore. See Sessions, workspaces and links.
