A virtual 4C profile is one row of the map: everything one locus, the anchor, touches, as a track along
the genome that you can export as a bigWig. It is computed once, over the anchor's sequence or the whole
genome, so panning does not change it.

## Running it

Open a contact map, choose **4C profile from an anchor** in the **2D tasks** card of the **Analysis**
panel, and name the **anchor**: type a locus, 1-based and inclusive as IGV and samtools write it, such as
`IV:1,200,001-1,210,000`, or a sequence's name; or press **pick on map** and Shift-drag along either
axis of the map, which fills the box instead of zooming. Leave **cis only** on to read the anchor's own
sequence, or turn it off for the whole genome. Press **run**.

The profile is a row of the **Tracks** card in the **Annotations** panel, marked `*` and named after its
anchor, *4C · IV:1,200,001-1,210,000*, then *(with trans)*, *· O/E*, *· per coverage* or *· anchor
excluded* as they apply. Its bigWig, bedGraph and TSV are in the row's settings. The same anchor run
again replaces its row.

## Parameters

**normalisation**: **by P(s)** divides each bin by what its separation from the anchor predicts on the
anchor's own sequence, taking distance out of the shape; **by coverage** divides it by how much the bin
is seen at all, centred on one, so a bin every contact misses is not a dip; **by P(s) and coverage** does
both; **none**, the default, is the contacts as counted, as in a 4C bigWig. **exclude anchor region**
leaves the anchor and one bin either side empty.

## Reading the result

**Raw**, a bin's value is its contacts with the anchor, summed over the anchor's bins, balanced where the
file has weights. On a log scale it is a triangle with its apex at the anchor, and the contacts you are
after are bumps on its flanks.

**Over P(s)**, the profile is centred on 1: a bin above 1 touches the anchor more than its distance
predicts. Off the anchor's sequence, the expected is the mean of what the anchor reaches on the other
sequences.

**Dips** left on a raw map are mostly coverage, such as repeats and bins with few restriction sites,
exactly where a spurious contact is most tempting. **by coverage** takes them out, most usefully on a raw
map, since balancing does much the same.

**The anchor's own bins** are the brightest by orders of magnitude and set an autoscaled track's scale;
**exclude anchor region** leaves them out.

**Gaps and zeros.** With **cis only** on, every bin off the anchor's sequence is a gap, not 0. A bin
with no stored contact with the anchor is 0, and a bin the balancer dropped is a gap.

## Compared with cooltools

In the validation, over the whole genome, the profile is `cooltools.virtual4c`'s to float32, except at
the viewpoint itself and where cooltools leaves a bin without a value: chromosomix writes 0 there.
