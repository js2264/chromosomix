A virtual 4C profile is one row of the map: everything one locus, the anchor, touches, as a track along
the genome. Reach for it to ask what a promoter, an enhancer or a boundary contacts, to read one locus's
contacts against the tracks beside it, or to take a viewpoint out of the app as a bigWig. The task reads
for itself, over the anchor's whole sequence or the whole genome, so the track is the same whatever was
on screen when you ran it.

## Running it

Open a contact map, then choose **4C profile from an anchor** in the **2D tasks** card of the
**Analysis** panel, and name the **anchor**:

- type a locus, 1-based and inclusive as IGV and samtools write them, such as `IV:1,200,001-1,210,000`,
  or a whole sequence by its name; or
- press **pick on map**, which then reads *pointing…*, and Shift-drag along either axis of the map: the
  drag fills the box instead of zooming, once, and the box stays editable.

Leave **cis only** on to read the anchor's own sequence: one dense square, a handful of range requests,
or no reads at all when the map on screen already holds it. Turn it off for the whole genome: one pass
over the pixel table as far as the anchor, about half the file, or all of it when the profile is divided
by coverage. Then press **run**.

The profile arrives as a row of the **Tracks** card in the **Annotations** panel, marked `*` and named
after its anchor and what was done to it: *4C · IV:1,200,001-1,210,000*, followed by *(with trans)*,
*· O/E*, *· per coverage* or *· anchor excluded* as they apply. Its bigWig, bedGraph and TSV are in the
row's settings. A profile from another anchor is a row of its own; the same anchor run again replaces
its row.

## Parameters

**normalisation** is one control with four answers, because its two divisions are independent. **by
P(s)** divides each bin by what its separation from the anchor predicts, from the decay of the anchor's
own sequence rather than the genome's, which takes distance out of the profile's shape. **by coverage**
divides each bin by how much it is seen at all, centred on one so that the profile keeps its units,
which stops a bin every contact misses reading as a dip. **by P(s) and coverage** does both, and **none**,
the default, is the contacts as counted, which is what a bigWig of a 4C experiment holds. **exclude
anchor region** leaves the anchor and one bin either side empty.

## Reading the result

**Raw**, a bin's value is its contacts with the anchor, summed over the anchor's bins, and balanced
wherever the file carries weights. Contact falls by orders of magnitude with distance, so on a log scale
a raw profile is a triangle with the anchor at its apex, and the contacts you ran it to find are bumps on
its flanks: plain near the anchor, invisible a megabase away.

**Over P(s)**, the profile is centred on 1, and a bin above 1 touches the anchor more than its distance
predicts, wherever it is. Off the anchor's sequence, with **cis only** off, the expected is the mean of
what the anchor reaches on the other sequences, so a profile with both halves carries one quantity.

**The dips that remain** on a raw map are mostly coverage: a bin in a repeat, a bin with few restriction
sites, a bin the mapper half lost is seen less by every anchor, and those dips sit exactly where a
spurious contact is most tempting to believe. **by coverage** takes them out. A balanced map has had
something much like it done already, which is what balancing is, so the division matters most on a raw
one.

**The anchor's own bins** are the brightest of the profile by orders of magnitude, its contact with
itself and its shoulder. On an autoscaled track they set the scale every other bin is read on; **exclude
anchor region** leaves them out, and the track's name says so.

**Gaps and zeros.** With **cis only** on, every bin off the anchor's sequence is a gap, not 0: the run
said nothing about it. A bin with no stored contact with the anchor is 0, and a bin the balancer dropped
is a gap.

## Things to know

- **A task, not a view.** The profile is computed once, for the anchor's whole sequence or the whole
  genome, and recorded with the framing it was run on; panning does not change it.
- **Validation.** Over the whole genome, at three viewpoints on a mouse Hi-C map at 10 kb (the promoters
  of *Sox2*, *Nanog* and *Pou5f1*), the profile is `cooltools.virtual4c`'s, to float32. The only bins
  that differ are those cooltools leaves without a value, where chromosomix writes 0 because the map
  holds no contact with the viewpoint, and the viewpoint itself.
