Before a map is read, the library behind it is judged by its records: how many read pairs mapped, how
many were duplicates, how much is cis and at what separations, and how short a separation its four read
orientations can be believed at. A library cut with a restriction enzyme raises one more question: how
many pairs are not a contact at all, but a fragment that was never ligated, closed on itself or joined
back to its neighbour. None of that survives binning, which sums the four orientations together and
forgets where in a bin a read ended, so this task reads the `.pairs` the map was made from and counts
what `pairtools stats` and `pairtools restrict` count.

## Running it

Open the map, then choose **Pairs QC** in the **2D tasks** card of the **Analysis** panel. Where the
records come from depends on how the map was opened:

- **A map binned from a `.pairs` as you opened it** had its records counted by that import. The
  statistics and the orientation curves cost nothing, and the form asks for no file.
- **A map opened as a matrix**, such as a `.cool`, an `.mcool` or a `.hic`, keeps no records, so the
  form asks for the `.pairs` it was made from: drop it on the form's drop area, click the area to choose
  it, or type its address in the box under it. It is read for its records alone: nothing is binned and
  the map does not change. **run** is refused until one is named.

Drop the `.pairs` on the form itself: dropped anywhere else in the window, it is opened as a map.

For the restriction statistics, load the reference the reads were mapped to, a FASTA or a `.2bit` (see
[Reference sequences](/files/sequences/)), and choose it and the library's enzyme under **Restriction
enzyme**. Pairs QC then runs [Restriction sites](/analyses/restriction/) first to cut the reference, and
reads every record against the cuts. With no reference, or the enzyme left at **none**, the run is the
pairtools counts alone.

The drawer shows the pairtools figures and their table, then, with a reference and an enzyme, four
figures of the restriction fragments.

The same run from a shell:

From a shell the `.pairs` can be the map itself, binned at the resolution `--bin-size` names, with the
reference loaded by `--sequence`:

```sh
chromosomix pairsqc sample.pairs.gz --bin-size 10000 --sequence ref.fa.gz --enzyme DpnII --out qc/
```

## Parameters

Two answers matter: the `.pairs`, where the map was not binned from one, and the **enzyme**, which must
be the one the library was cut with, since every class below is read against its cuts. **Arima** stands
for its kit's two enzymes, and **custom** takes enzymes by name, aliases such as MboI included, or sites
typed with a `^` where they cut. A Micro-C or Omni-C library is cut by a nuclease, not an enzyme: leave
**enzyme** at **none**.

## Reading the result

### The pairtools counts

- **P(s) by pair orientation**: the decay of the cis records in each of the four orientations, `++`,
  `+-`, `-+` and `--`, all four divided by one shared total. Ligation does not know which way a fragment
  pointed, so at long range the four lie on top of each other; at short range they part, where
  self-circles, dangling ends and undigested fragments outnumber contacts. The separation where they meet
  is the shortest the library can be read at, and it is usually shorter than one bin of the map. The
  run's note gives it by pairtools' rule — past the last separation at which one orientation strays more
  than 5 % from the mean of the four — and warns that a sparse tail of chance strays can put it further
  out than the curves suggest.
- **Pair types**: how many records had each pairtools pair type, most common first.
- **Cis and trans**: the shares of the mapped, non-duplicate pairs that are cis and trans, and cis at
  least 1, 2, 4, 10, 20 and 40 kb apart, on a scale of 0 to 1 and named by pairtools' own keys.
- **pairtools stats**: every entry `pairtools stats` writes but its three long lists, in its keys and its
  spelling of a number, so that a row can be laid beside a line of a `.stats` file.

### The restriction fragments

With a reference and an enzyme, both ends of every mapped, non-duplicate pair are placed in the fragments
between the enzyme's cuts, and each pair takes a class from its two ends' fragments and strands, the ends
in order along the sequence:

| Class | The two ends |
| --- | --- |
| valid | anything not below: a contact |
| re-ligation | in two neighbouring fragments, `+-`: a fragment joined back to its neighbour, or a site left uncut |
| dangling | in one fragment, `+-`: a fragment that was never ligated |
| self-circle | in one fragment, `-+`: a fragment closed on itself |
| same-strand | in one fragment, `++` or `--` |
| trans | on two sequences |

Four figures follow:

- **Pair classes by orientation**: the classes as bars stacked by the two ends' strands, followed by the
  same counts as a table, with each class's share of the pairs placed.
- **Distance from a read to the next cut**, in the direction the read points, as a histogram per strand.
  A dangling end's two reads both give its fragment's length, so the two strands lie together.
- **Read ends per fragment**: how many ends each of the reference's fragments holds, in powers of two,
  with how many fragments hold any at all in the figure's title.
- **Coverage against sites**: each bin of the map, its raw contacts against the enzyme's cuts in it, with
  Spearman's ρ between the two in the title. The mean coverage for a number of cuts is drawn only where at
  least five bins share it, so that one bright bin in a repeat does not draw the line.

## Things to know

- **Each end is placed by its own strand's cut.** An enzyme that leaves an overhang cuts the bottom strand
  a few bases from the top strand's cut, and a `-` read starts on the bottom strand: for DpnII its end lies
  3 bp past the top strand's cut. Placed by the top strand's cuts alone, as one run of `pairtools restrict`
  places every end, a DpnII library's dangling ends fall in two neighbouring fragments and are counted as
  re-ligations. Pairs QC places a `+` end by the top strand's cuts and a `-` end by the bottom strand's.
- **Validation.** On a yeast Hi-C contact list against DpnII sites, at 5 kb, the statistics are
  `pairtools stats`' key for key, and the classes, distances, fragments seen and coverage against sites
  are those of `pairtools restrict`, run once per strand, and of `cooltools.coverage`: every count is the
  same.
