Inter-arm alignment turns the secondary diagonal of a bacterial map into numbers. Where SMC is loaded at *parS* beside the origin and zips the two arms together as it travels, each locus touches its mirror image on the other arm, and the map carries a second diagonal through the origin, perpendicular to the main one. It is present in *Bacillus subtilis*, lost without ParB or SMC, and absent from *Escherichia coli*, whose MukBEF does not align the arms (Wang et al. 2017). This task says how strongly a chromosome's arms are held together and how far from the origin, so a species, or a mutant and its wild type, can be compared by a number rather than by an impression of a picture.

## Running it

The arms are read from the origin, so tell the app where it is and that the chromosome is a circle. Both are columns of the Chromosomes table, reached from the first step of the breadcrumb (see [Moving around](/browse/navigating/)): tick the sequence's **circular** box, and type its **origin**, 1-based — or run [Origin and terminus](/analyses/oriter/), which fills the origin in for you. Until one is set the origin is the sequence's first base, which is where a complete bacterial assembly conventionally starts, at *dnaA*, and the drawer says that is what it used.

Then open the **Analysis** panel (<kbd>4</kbd>), choose **Inter-arm alignment** in the **2D tasks** card, and press **run**. What comes back:

- **Inter-arm enrichment**, a track of the **Tracks** card in the **Annotations** panel, marked `*`: how much each locus touches its mirror image, in log2;
- in the drawer, **Inter-arm alignment by distance from the origin**, a curve per sequence — the figure the literature draws — and the same **after detrend** beside it, on the same axis;
- **Contact decay along the diagonal and along both arms**, the expectations the two curves are read over;
- **Inter-arm alignment per sequence**, a table with one row per chromosome.

![The B. subtilis wild-type map at 10 kb framed on its origin, the secondary diagonal crossing the main one there, Inter-arm enrichment in a lane to its left, and the drawer beside it on Inter-arm alignment by distance from the origin, rising from below 0 at the origin to about 1 within 500 kb.](../../../assets/screens/analyses/interarm-origin-curve.webp)

The same run from a shell, where `--circular` and `--origin` declare what the Chromosomes table holds in the app:

<TaskCommand id="interarm" />

```sh
chromosomix interarm ecoli.mcool::/resolutions/5000 --circular NC_000913.3 \
  --origin NC_000913.3:3925744 --out results/
```

## Parameters

**Band** is how close to the secondary diagonal a cell must be to count, in bins either side of it; 0 is the diagonal alone. It also sets how much is left out near the origin and the terminus, where the two diagonals meet (below). **Window along the arms** belongs only to the control (below), and is Compartments' and the map's own 50 bins unless you change it.

## Reading the result

### The measurement

Each cell's observed over expected is taken over the sequence's own distance decay — the ordinary one, which is exactly what makes the secondary diagonal stand out — and its log2 averaged within **band** bins of the secondary diagonal, by distance from the origin. Near the origin and the terminus the two diagonals run into each other, and a cell there is a pair a few bins apart along the DNA whose ratio says how compact the stretch is rather than whether the arms are aligned; so a cell nearer the main diagonal than twice the band and two bins is left out.

On the curve, 0 is no alignment: mirror images touching as often as any two loci as far apart along the DNA. An aligned chromosome stands above it out from the origin, and falls back where the alignment ends.

### The table

- **read as**: circular or linear.
- **origin**: the origin the arms were read from, 1-based.
- **enrichment**: how many times as often mirror images touch as expected, from the origin out to the reach — 1 is no alignment, 2 is twice as often. It is two to the mean log2, so enrichment and depletion weigh alike; where nothing reaches, it is the whole arm's.
- **enrichment after detrend**: the same cells over the decay along both arms, which takes the secondary diagonal into itself, read over the same stretch. It is a control on that detrending, not a second measure of the alignment: near 1 where the detrending works.
- **reach**: how far from the origin, in base pairs, the enrichment holds before it falls four-fifths of the way from its peak back to 1, on a log scale — where the alignment ends rather than the middle of its fall. The peak and the reach are read from a running mean over 2% of the arm, so one noisy distance neither sets the peak nor carries the reach to the terminus.
- **arm**: the length of the longer arm.

So the reach says how far the arms are held, and the enrichment how strongly where they are. As a guide, a *Pseudomonas aeruginosa* map, whose arms are aligned, reads 3.1 to 3.2 at resolutions from 2 to 16 kb, reaching about 3.0 Mb of its 3.2 Mb arms, and 0.93 to 0.97 after detrend; *E. coli*, whose arms are not aligned, reads 1.20 to 1.22. An unaligned chromosome does not read exactly 1, because the stretch its enrichment is read over is wherever its noise runs highest.

### The curves

**After detrend** is drawn on the same ordinate as the measurement, so a flat line looks flat rather than like a profile fitted to its own noise. **Contact decay along the diagonal and along both arms** shows what each cell was divided by: the ordinary decay along the diagonal, the short way round a circle, which the enrichment is read over; and the decay along both arms — within an arm, and across the arms by distance from the secondary diagonal — which the control is read over.

### What to watch for

- **Declare the circle.** A chromosome read as linear, with its origin near either end, has one arm; the drawer says so.
- **Size.** Each chromosome's square is read whole, so one past 8,192 bins is left out and named: open the file at a coarser resolution. One under 8 bins has no arms to speak of and is left out too.
- **Comparing libraries.** On a deck, **run on all N maps** brings the tables together as one, with a column naming the map each row was measured on: every library's enrichment side by side. See [Maps side by side](/browse/deck/).

This measure follows no published package, so it has no badge and no section of the validation.
