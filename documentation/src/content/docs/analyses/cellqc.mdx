The cells of a single-cell Hi-C library differ less by depth than by where in the cell cycle they were
fixed, and a pseudo-bulk summed across phases smears the very structure you opened the file to see. The
**Cells** table can rank cells only by what the file records about them, such as their stored pixels
and contacts, which describes the sequencing more than the contacts. This task measures each cell of an
`.scool` from its own contacts instead: its cis and trans shares, and the share of its contacts in four
bands of separation, Nagano et al. (2017)'s, whose near band against its mitotic band draws the cell
cycle as a horseshoe. Reach for it before you tick the cells to sum.

## Running it

Open an `.scool`; it opens on its **Cells** table. **Per-cell contact QC** is in the **2D tasks** card
of the **Analysis** panel, and only for such a file. Leave **cells** on **every cell in the file**, since
this is the answer that helps you choose, or pick **the ticked ones only**, and press **run**. The run
is one pass over each cell's own pixels: the same reads that summing those cells costs.

The drawer shows a table with a row per cell and the phasing scatter, a point per cell. The cells'
shares also arrive as columns of the **Cells** table, where they sort like any other column: sort by the
near or the mitotic share, tick a run of cells, and sum them.

## Parameters

The three band edges are in base pairs, unlike every other setting about the matrix, because they are
the paper's numbers and the axes of its figure: the near band runs from 23 kb to 2 Mb, the mitotic band
from 2 Mb to 12 Mb, and everything beyond is the far band. They are sized for mouse; on a smaller genome,
set them to its scale.

## Reading the result

### The table

A row per cell: its name, whether it is **Ticked**, its **Contacts** and **Stored pixels**, its **Cis**
and **Trans** shares, and the share of its contacts in each band, headed by the band's edges — `< 23 kb`,
`23 kb–2 Mb`, `2 Mb–12 Mb` and `> 12 Mb` at the defaults. Every share is of all the cell's contacts, trans
included, so that a cell with an unusual trans share is not made to look ordinary. A contact within one
bin has a separation of 0 and counts in the first band, among the self-ligation and unligated ends that
band is there to keep apart from structure. A cell with no contact at all has blank shares, not zeros.

### The phasing scatter

Each point is a cell, its near share across and its mitotic share up. Across a population the cells
fall on a horseshoe, and where a cell lies along it is where it was in the cycle: cells just after
mitosis carry a large mitotic band and little near contact, cells in G1 the reverse, and cells in S and
G2 lie between. The task does not assign a phase or cluster the cells: where to cut the horseshoe is a
choice, and it is yours, made by ticking cells in the **Cells** table.

## Things to know

- **Small genomes.** On an assembly whose longest sequence lies inside the near band — a yeast, for
  one — the mitotic and far bands are empty by construction and the scatter says nothing. The run's note
  says so; set the band edges to that assembly's scale.
- **Not yet validated.** The bands are Nagano et al.'s, but the [validation](/analyses/validation/) does
  not yet compare this task with a reference, so its badge is not green.
