This task measures each cell of an `.scool` from its own contacts: its cis and trans shares, and its
shares in the four bands of separation of Nagano et al. (2017), whose near band against its mitotic band
draws the cell cycle as a horseshoe. Run it before you tick the cells to sum: a pseudo-bulk summed across
phases smears the structure you opened the file to see.

## Running it

Open an `.scool`, which opens on its **Cells** table, choose **Per-cell contact QC** in the **2D tasks**
card of the **Analysis** panel, leave **cells** on **every cell in the file** or pick **the ticked ones
only**, and press **run**. The drawer shows a table with a row per cell and the phasing scatter. The
shares also arrive as columns of the **Cells** table: sort by the near or the mitotic share, tick a run
of cells, and sum them.

## Reading the result

The table has a row per cell: its name, whether it is **Ticked**, its **Contacts** and **Stored pixels**,
its **Cis** and **Trans** shares, and its share in each band, headed by the band's edges — `< 23 kb`,
`23 kb–2 Mb`, `2 Mb–12 Mb` and `> 12 Mb` at the defaults. Every share is of all the cell's contacts,
trans included. A contact within one bin has a separation of 0 and counts in the first band. A cell with
no contact has blank shares, not zeros.

In the phasing scatter each point is a cell, its near share across and its mitotic share up. Across a
population the cells fall on a horseshoe, and where a cell lies along it is where it was in the cycle:
just after mitosis a large mitotic band and little near contact, in G1 the reverse, and S and G2 between.
The task assigns no phase: where to cut the horseshoe is yours, by ticking cells in the **Cells** table.

On an assembly whose longest sequence lies inside the near band, such as a yeast, the mitotic and far
bands are empty and the scatter says nothing; the run's note says so. Set the band edges to its scale.
