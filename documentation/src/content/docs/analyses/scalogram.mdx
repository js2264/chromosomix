The scalogram shows how far each bin's contacts reach: at every bin, the separation within which a
tenth, a quarter, a half, three quarters or nine tenths of its contacts lie, drawn along the genome as
nested bands, as Lioy et al. (2018) and HiContacts' `scalogram()` draw it. Where P(s) averages a sequence
into one curve, the scalogram shows which stretch folds differently from the rest.

## Running it

Open a contact map, choose **Scalogram** in the **2D tasks** card of the **Analysis** panel, tick the
sequences, choose the quantiles and press **run**. The bands arrive on the **Tracks** card of the
**Annotations** panel, a row each — *Scalogram 10 %*, *Scalogram 25 %* and so on — grouped as
**Scalogram** in one lane, on a logarithmic axis in base pairs.

## Parameters

**look out to**, at 0, reads each sequence whole: quick on a bacterial or a yeast map, a large read on a
mammalian chromosome, and the only setting at which a band is a quantile of all of a bin's contacts. Any
other reach, in bins, makes each band a quantile of what lies within it, and its name says so:
*Scalogram 50 % · within 500 kb*. **diagonals to ignore** leaves out each bin's contact with itself,
which would pull every band towards zero; **minimum coverage of the distribution** leaves a bin without
bands where too few of its separations have a value.

## Reading the result

Each band is a separation: at the 50 % band, half of everything the bin touches lies closer than the
band's height. A low, narrow bundle is a stretch whose contacts are local; a high one, a stretch whose
contacts reach far; a step in every band at one place, a boundary between two ways of folding. The
median does not move when the balancer drops one distant cell, where the mean can move by a factor of
two, so a deep map and a shallow one compare.

A hole in the bands is a bin with too little of its distribution to measure, such as beside a stretch the
balancer dropped; a band exists only where the one below it does too.

### What to watch for

- **The order of the rows is the picture.** Each band fills down to the one below it in the group:
  reorder them and the picture changes.
- **Circular sequences.** A separation is the shorter way round, at most half the sequence.
- **Short sequences**, of fewer than four bins, are left out, with a note.
- **The values are base pairs.** Only the lane is logarithmic; the bigWig, bedGraph and TSV hold the
  separations as computed.
