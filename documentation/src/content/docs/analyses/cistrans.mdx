The first number most Hi-C papers quote, per sequence: how much of each chromosome's contact stays on it
(cis) and how much reaches the others (trans). Beside it, a square of every sequence against every other
says which pairs touch more than their depth predicts. Reach for it to see whether a library worked and
whether the karyotype is what the assembly says: a pair well above the rest is a translocation, a
fusion, a centromere cluster or a Rabl configuration, depending on the organism.

## Running it

Open a contact map, then choose **Cis and trans per sequence** in the **2D tasks** card of the
**Analysis** panel. Every sequence is ticked; untick the ones you want left out of the results, and
press **run**. A sequence's share of its own contact needs every contact touching it, so
the run is one pass over the whole pixel table whatever is ticked, unless the whole file is already on
screen at its own resolution and balanced the same way.

The drawer shows four results, from top to bottom: a table with a row per sequence, the same sequences'
cis fractions as bars, a ranked table of sequence pairs, and the square between sequences.

## Parameters

**off-diagonal value** decides what the pairs table and the square show. **against indifference**, the
default, is the contacts between two sequences over what the two sequences' own trans contacts predict,
scaled so that the expectation adds up to the trans contacts there are: 1 is what any two sequences of
that depth would give, and a fusion is far above it. **raw contacts** is the count, and **per bin
pair** the count divided by the two sequences' bin counts. **least contact worth listing** keeps a pair
with only a few stray reads out of the top of the table, and **bars per sequence** draws the cis
fraction, the trans fraction, or both stacked.

## Reading the result

### Per sequence

The table lists the sequences in the file's own order, the order the map is drawn in, each with its
**Bins**, its **Cis**, **Trans** and **Total** contacts, its **Cis fraction** (the share of the
contacts touching it that stay on it), **Trans / cis**, and **Contacts per bin**. The bars draw the cis
fraction of each sequence in the same order, on a scale of 0 to 100 %, so a sequence that behaves unlike
the others stands above or below its neighbours before you read a number. They are fractions rather than
counts because a bar of contacts would only show how long each sequence is. A sequence with no contact
at all has no fraction: it is a gap among the bars and a blank in the table, never 0.

### Between sequences

The square has a row and a column per ticked sequence, against indifference coloured blue below 1 and
red above it, centred on 1. Each sequence against itself is left out, being one to three orders of
magnitude above every other cell and so the whole colour scale. The axes name the sequences when there
are forty or fewer. A cell well above 1 is a pair of sequences that touch more than their depths
predict.

The pairs table ranks the same pairs, strongest first: both sequences, their raw **Contacts**, and,
against indifference, the **Expected** count beside **Observed / expected**. It lists the strongest 500
by default, and says how many pairs passed the threshold when there are more.

## Things to know

- **A summary, not a caller.** Pairs are ranked against a stated null with no significance attached:
  the table says which pairs stand out, not that a translocation is there.
- **Balanced where the file is.** The sums are of balanced contacts wherever the file carries weights;
  `--no-balance` reads the raw counts. The [map report](/analyses/mapreport/) runs this task on the raw
  counts.
- **The square stops at 512 sequences.** Past that its cells are too small to be a picture, the run
  says so, and the pairs table is the answer; the bars have no such limit.
- **Validation.** On a chicken map of 36 sequences, from the macrochromosomes to microchromosomes a few
  bins long, every sum is the one cooltools' `expected_cis` and `expected_trans` give, raw and balanced,
  to the digits written, and every ratio to indifference is NumPy's over those sums.
