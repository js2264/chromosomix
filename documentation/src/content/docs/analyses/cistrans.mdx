How much of each chromosome's contact stays on it (cis) and how much reaches the others (trans), and
which pairs of sequences touch more than their depth predicts: whether a library worked, and whether the
karyotype is what the assembly says. A pair well above the rest is a translocation, a fusion, a
centromere cluster or a Rabl configuration, depending on the organism.

## Running it

Open a contact map, choose **Cis and trans per sequence** in the **2D tasks** card of the **Analysis**
panel, untick any sequence to leave out of the results, and press **run**. The drawer shows the tables,
the bars and the square below.

## Parameters

**off-diagonal value** decides what the pairs table and the square show. **against indifference**, the
default, is the contacts between two sequences over what their own trans contacts predict: 1 is what any
two sequences of that depth would give, and a fusion is far above it. **raw contacts** is the count, and
**per bin pair** the count divided by the two sequences' bin counts. **least contact worth listing** keeps
a pair with a few stray reads out of the top of the table, and **bars per sequence** draws the cis
fraction, the trans fraction, or both stacked.

## Reading the result

### Per sequence

The table lists the sequences in the file's order, each with its **Bins**, its **Cis**, **Trans** and
**Total** contacts, its **Cis fraction** (the share of its contacts that stay on it), **Trans / cis** and
**Contacts per bin**. The bars draw the cis fractions in the same order, from 0 to 100 %. A sequence
with no contact has no fraction: a gap among the bars and a blank in the table, never 0.

### Between sequences

The square has a row and a column per ticked sequence, against indifference coloured blue below 1 and
red above it; each sequence against itself, orders of magnitude above the rest, is left out. A cell well
above 1 is a pair of sequences that touch more than their depths predict.

The pairs table ranks the same pairs, strongest first: both sequences, their raw **Contacts** and, against
indifference, the **Expected** count beside **Observed / expected**. It says how many pairs passed the
threshold when there are more than it lists.

### What to watch for

- **A summary, not a caller.** Pairs are ranked against a stated null with no significance attached: a
  pair that stands out is not proof of a translocation.
- **Balanced where the file is**, unless you pass `--no-balance`. The [map report](/analyses/mapreport/)
  runs this task on the raw counts.
- **The square stops at 512 sequences**, and the run says so; the pairs table and the bars have no such
  limit.
