Do two replicates agree? Does an insulation score follow a ChIP-seq track? **Track correlation** gives the correlation of every pair of the tracks you choose, over fixed windows along the genome or over the features of a set.

## Running it

Load two or more tracks ([Tracks and features](/files/tracks/)), open **Track correlation** on the **1D tasks** card of the **Analysis** panel and tick the **tracks**; no map is needed. The drawer shows the correlation matrix, on a colour scale centred on 0, and **Pairs**, every pair from the most correlated to the least.

## Parameters

**compare over** is the question. **fixed windows** asks whether the tracks agree everywhere, an answer the empty majority of the genome dominates; **a feature set** asks whether they agree where something is happening, usually what was meant. **window**, 100 bp by default, is the width at which a coverage track says something about a peak; wider windows smear a peak into its neighbourhood. **method** is Spearman by default, which ranks the values first, so one very deep locus cannot carry the coefficient; Pearson correlates the values themselves.

## Reading the result

A coefficient near 1 is two tracks that rise and fall together; near 0, no relation; below 0, one is high where the other is low. Each unit's value is the exact mean over the bases the track has a value for, and over fixed windows each sequence's last, shorter window is left out.

A unit any one track lacks is left out of every pair, as in deepTools' `plotCorrelation`, so every coefficient is over the same units: one track with gaps, such as an insulation score where the map was sparse, narrows every pair. The note says how many units were left out, and **compared** beside **units** under the matrix how many are left; with fewer than three, no pair has a coefficient. To compare two tracks over everything they share, run them on their own.

## Compared with deepTools

[The validation](https://chromosomix.app/validation/#track-correlation-deeptools) holds the coefficients to pyBigWig's exact window means. deepTools reads a window's mean from a zoom level where the file has one fine enough, which moves its coefficients at the wider windows; at 100 bp it agrees to the digits it writes, hence *equivalent* rather than identical.
